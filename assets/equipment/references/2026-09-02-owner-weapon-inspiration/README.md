## Accepted review findings

- Cross-rank consistency must not collapse material changes into palette swaps.
- A new material should visibly affect construction, silhouette, proportion, detailing, or some
  combination of those qualities.
- Weapons, Shields, Bows, and Staffs may vary substantially across materials while retaining an
  immediately readable mechanical class.
- Armour must remain internally consistent as a full set and four derived pieces. Separate Armour
  materials do not need to reuse the same cut or silhouette.
- The completed v2 root images remain review evidence. They are not approved or runtime assets.

## Recommended direction pending owner acceptance

Use **controlled variation**, not rigid templates and not a combinatorial catalog of subfamilies:

1. Keep the camera, framing, pose, and normalized footprint stable within an item class so comparison
   remains easy.
2. Give every material one dominant construction idea and one unmistakable silhouette cue. A color
   change alone never satisfies the material change.
3. Keep F- and E-rank forms practical but appealing. Increase shape ambition and ornament with
   material rank, reserving the most extreme geometry for A, S, named, or monster-derived items.
4. Test recognition at phone-thumbnail size. If the class and material character disappear at about
   128 CSS pixels, the design relies too heavily on surface detail.
5. Do not add Round Shield, Composite Bow, or similar mechanical classes merely to obtain visual
   variety. Those can remain visual forms inside Shield or Bow until gameplay requires a distinction.

### Resolution recommendation

Retain 1024×1024 isolated-item masters and 1024×1536 full-set masters. For an initial mobile runtime,
derive 512×512 isolated items and 512×768 full sets; keep the 1024-pixel versions only for zoomed
inspection, larger screens, or future re-export. This preserves useful source detail without making
every phone download pay for master resolution.
