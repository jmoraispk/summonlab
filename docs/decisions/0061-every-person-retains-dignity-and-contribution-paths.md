# 0061 — Every person retains dignity and contribution paths

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-08-29

## Context

Summonlab deliberately separates rarity from eventual worth, but a systems-focused town also needs
to represent unequal starting talents, task allocation, specialization, and opportunity cost. That
can produce a rich argument for social design, or it can accidentally reduce less gifted people to
disposable labor and turn capability into a human-value score.

## Decision

- Every generated person has intrinsic dignity independent of genetics, body, aptitude, rarity,
  education, intelligence-related checks, obedience, or productivity.
- The game may represent unequal capabilities and learning rates without representing unequal human
  worth.
- A well-designed town finds meaningful contributions through capability, preference, goals,
  relationships, opportunity, mentorship, scarcity, growth, and lived perspective—not only peak
  output.
- Specialists have opportunity costs, and other people can own routine or overlooked work, improve
  it, and develop expertise. This is revisable comparative advantage, not permanent caste.
- Insight remains a mechanical attribute for explicit checks, learning, perception, and evidence;
  there is no IQ, genetic quality, or worth statistic.
- All heroes can learn non-exclusive skills. They may request development or reassignment, refuse,
  change goals, become bored, or react when a system undervalues them.
- Quiet civic contributions, teaching, knowledge, relationships, and institutional memory must
  remain mechanically legible enough that higher rarity does not erase them.
- Systems may portray exploitation, but its personal and institutional consequences remain causal;
  the game does not endorse it through a hidden optimal bonus.

## Consequences

Progression, jobs, parties, petitions, facilities, and journals require broader contribution models
than combat power. Synthesis loses future labor, teaching, coverage, knowledge, relationships, and
institutional resilience that immediate grants cannot copy. Character generation and evaluation
continue to reject correlations among protected appearance, personality, morality, intelligence,
obedience, and rarity.
