# 0059 — Player mastery is designing motivating systems

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-08-29

## Context

Autonomous heroes can train, work, socialize, rest, or refuse, but autonomy alone does not create
purpose. Without credible objectives, suitable challenge, feedback, facilities, leadership,
mentorship, recovery, or social meaning, boredom and purposeless idling are plausible outcomes. A
leadership game would become shallow if the player's answer were direct behavioral control or a
hidden productivity bonus.

## Decision

- Summonlab is fundamentally about designing systems that motivate autonomous agents toward useful
  goals.
- Facilities, schedules, party identity, leadership, mentorship, objectives, challenge fit,
  feedback, recognition, rewards, recovery, and social norms are causal inputs to hero decisions,
  habits, affect, and goals.
- Training and work remain voluntary legal behaviors influenced by those systems. The Master cannot
  set an obedience or motivation value directly.
- Idling, lazing around, boredom, avoidance, overwork, and loss of purpose remain valid systemic
  outcomes with inspectable causes.
- Petitions, conversations, activity projections, and the journal surface those causes so the
  player can redesign the environment, negotiate, or accept a different culture.
- System quality contributes to party readiness alongside level, skills, gear, knowledge, health,
  and cohesion.

## Consequences

Settlement buildings cannot be passive bonus containers; they must change affordances, friction,
capacity, schedules, feedback, or social contact. Affect/goals need canonical state and deterministic
updates. Policies need bounded motivation context, and evaluation fixtures must compare how the same
hero behaves under different institutional designs. Playtests should ask whether players understand
why behavior changed and whether redesigning a system feels more powerful than micromanaging one
hero at a time.
