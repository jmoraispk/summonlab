# Every person can contribute

Wiki ID: `principles.every-person-contributes`

Every generated hero has intrinsic dignity and a potential place in the town. Rarity, starting
capacity, body, aptitude, education, personality, or current productivity never determine whether a
life is worthwhile.

The game does not need to pretend that everyone begins with identical talent. Its stronger claim is
that a well-designed society can discover meaningful contributions from very different people,
develop their capacities, and benefit from perspectives that would never emerge if only the most
obviously gifted were allowed to act.

## System principle

Role allocation considers more than peak output:

- current capability and learned skills;
- affinity and physical constraints where mechanically relevant;
- interest, goals, values, and willingness;
- relationships, mentorship, trust, and team fit;
- need for practice and future growth;
- fatigue, health, risk, and recovery;
- scarcity, opportunity cost, and who else is available;
- the knowledge and perspective produced by someone's lived history.

A highly specialized hero may be best reserved for work only they can do. Another person can own a
routine or overlooked responsibility, improve it through repetition, build relationships around it,
and eventually become the town's expert. That is comparative advantage and development—not a claim
that less talented people exist to perform degrading work.

## Guardrails

- There is no player-facing IQ, worth, genetic quality, or human-value score.
- A hidden general cognitive factor can change comprehension-heavy practice gain by at most ten
  percent in either direction. Separate martial, magical, crafting, survival, and social aptitudes
  contribute by domain. Neither changes model reasoning, action quality, human worth, or eventual
  access.
- Appearance, ancestry, culture, gender, rarity, personality, morality, obedience, and intelligence
  remain independent generation dimensions.
- Any hero can learn any non-exclusive skill, though pace and domain fit may differ with aptitude,
  teachers, facilities, habits, health, and time. Aptitude does not set an eventual ceiling.
- Roles are revisable. Assignment must not silently become hereditary caste or permanent destiny.
- Heroes can prefer, refuse, negotiate, become bored, seek recognition, request training, or change
  goals.
- Routine work must still have safety, social meaning, feedback, and paths to mastery.
- The system may portray exploitative institutions, but it must make their human consequences real
  rather than endorse them through a hidden optimality bonus.

## Why uniqueness emerges

Uniqueness is not only a generator trait. It accumulates through:

```text
starting capacities
+ assigned opportunities
+ practiced tasks and learned skills
+ relationships and mentorship
+ witnessed events and private knowledge
+ habits, mistakes, injuries, successes, and goals
= a perspective no replacement possesses
```

This is why a person is not a disposable duplicate even when another summon has higher starting
numbers. Removing them also removes their future development, social edges, practical memory,
trust, and the part of the town built around their participation.

## Player mastery

The Master should succeed by designing systems that match people to useful opportunities while
helping them grow:

- clear shared objectives without mandatory sameness;
- legible roles and paths to new roles;
- mentoring and teaching networks;
- facilities and tools that reduce unnecessary friction;
- challenge appropriate to current capability;
- feedback, recognition, rest, and social belonging;
- leaders who surface hidden capacity and dissatisfaction;
- redundancy for survival without treating people as interchangeable.

An efficient system that destroys dignity, learning, trust, resilience, or future capacity is not
actually optimal over the life of the settlement.

## Mechanical implications

- Facility and party systems need more roles than direct combat damage.
- Repetition can build specialized skill, habit, local knowledge, and process improvement.
- Mentoring creates value for both teacher and learner without copying full mastery.
- Petitions must allow overlooked heroes to request tools, training, responsibility, or reassignment.
- Readiness includes coverage, resilience, knowledge, and cohesion rather than summing rarity.
- Synthesis loses social and institutional capital that immediate XP cannot replace.
- The journal should make quiet contributions visible enough that the player can recognize them.

## Open questions

- How should routine civic mastery become visible without turning every task into an XP bar?
- How do leaders discover underused capability without omnisciently reading hidden potential?
- Which tasks can be redesigned or automated as the town grows, freeing people for new development?
- How should a hero react when they believe the system consistently undervalues them?
