## Reading the race briefs

These remain **source briefs**, not accepted species canon or runtime statistics. The original
wording proposes biology, culture, cognition, hostility, rank, and personhood; it does not establish
universal facts. Apply the [classification axes](#classification-axes) before promoting an entry to
canon. The 150 source positions informed the canonical Initiate, Soldier, Specialist, Champion,
and Sovereign forms without changing the source-bound production drafts.

The [Goblinoid warbands](#goblinoid-warbands) section separately records accepted encounter rules,
unresolved family lore, and candidate artwork. The [source guide](generator-bestiary-guide.md)
remains an internal production reference.

## 1. Goblins

**What they are.** Small, wiry humanoids roughly the size of a child, with green or gray skin, oversized pointed ears, hooked noses and mouths crowded with crooked teeth. They live in filthy warrens, caves and sewers in large numbers, and are individually weak but overwhelming in a mob. Goblins build nothing of their own — every tool, weapon and scrap of armor they carry was stolen, scavenged from a battlefield, or hammered together from broken pieces of somebody else's property. Their culture is a pecking order enforced by violence, and the biggest, luckiest and most heavily looted goblin rules the rest.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Short, hunched, long-armed, oversized head and ears, disproportionately large hands and feet.
- **Palette:** Sickly green and gray-green skin, muddy browns, rust-orange, dull iron. Accents of stolen gold.
- **Material:** Rags, patched leather, cracked wood, rusted scrap metal, bone, rope.
- **Ornamentation:** Trophies and loot — mismatched helmets, coin necklaces, teeth, stolen banners.
- **Escalation axis:** Scrap becomes treasure, and the posture straightens from a cringing crouch to an upright swagger.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Runt | Knock-kneed and cringing, filthy loincloth, bare feet, sharpened stick. Almost no gear. |
| T2 | Cutter | Stolen helmet three sizes too big, mismatched leather scraps, notched cleaver, small coin pouch. |
| T3 | Raider | Standing nearly upright. Riveted plate scavenged from three different armies, a real sword, a shield made from a door. |
| T4 | Warboss | Hulking for a goblin, gold-toothed, wearing a chestplate looted from a knight, dragging a banner of enemy helms. |
| T5 | Goblin King | Enthroned on welded loot, crown of fused crowns, cloak stitched from captured flags, rings on every finger, dais of coins. |

## 2. Orcs

**What they are.** Tall, heavily muscled humanoids with gray, green or ashen skin, jutting lower jaws and prominent tusks that grow up from the bottom lip. They are a warrior species organized into clans, where status is earned in battle and displayed openly on the body — war paint, scars, piercings, and trophies taken from the fallen. Orcs are strong enough that they favor heavy, brutal weapons over finesse, and their armor tends to be thick, riveted iron rather than anything elegant. A clan is led by whoever can physically dominate the others.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Broad, top-heavy, thick-necked, slightly forward-leaning stance. Wide shoulders, heavy jaw.
- **Palette:** Gray-green and ash-gray skin, blood red, black, dark iron, bone white. War paint in red and white.
- **Material:** Boiled leather, fur, riveted iron, bone, crude steel.
- **Ornamentation:** War paint, ritual scarring, tusk rings, skull trophies, spiked pauldrons, clan totems.
- **Escalation axis:** Muscle mass and tusk length — tusks grow longer and curve further up the face with each tier.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Grunt | Lean and sinewy, hide wraps, small blunt tusks, crude spear or hand axe. |
| T2 | Warrior | Broad-shouldered, red war paint across the eyes, iron-banded leather, heavy axe, first scars. |
| T3 | Berserker | Slab-muscled, tusks curling past the cheekbones, spiked pauldrons, twin cleavers, scar-lattice across the chest. |
| T4 | Warchief | Towering and thick as a door, tusks capped in iron, plate armor over fur, greataxe in each hand, skull totem on the back. |
| T5 | Overlord | Near-giant. Tusks the length of daggers, armor forged from a great beast's ribcage, cloak of hides, banner-pole of enemy standards. |

## 3. Giants

**What they are.** Enormous humanoids, anywhere from twice to fifty times the height of a person, built along the same basic lines as humans but far heavier in bone and limb. They live in mountains, glaciers and deep valleys, and the oldest of them are so still and so long-lived that stone, moss and even trees begin to grow on their bodies. A giant does not need weapons in any refined sense — an uprooted tree or a chained boulder does the job. The largest are less like people and more like landscape that has decided to walk.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human proportions scaled up, but with heavier brow, thicker limbs and a slight stoop under its own weight.
- **Palette:** Stone gray, granite blue-gray, earth brown, moss green, glacier white. Warm ember tones in the eyes.
- **Material:** Rough hide, raw stone, moss, lichen, pine, iron chain, rope.
- **Ornamentation:** Chains, standing-stone jewelry, carved runes on the skin, whole trees used as decoration.
- **Escalation axis:** Raw scale, plus how much geology has spread across the body.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Brute | Twice human height. Rough hide clothes, a tree-trunk club, bare weathered skin. |
| T2 | Hill Giant | Four times human height. Moss on the shoulders, first stone plates forming along the back, rope belt. |
| T3 | Stone Giant | House-sized. Boulder-scale ridges down the arms, cliff-face brow, a megalith swung on a chain. |
| T4 | Mountain Lord | Mountain-tall. Waterfalls running off the shoulders, pines growing in the crags of its back, iron rings the size of gates. |
| T5 | Primordial Titan | A walking range. An entire valley on its back, glaciers in its beard, eyes like furnace mouths, clouds at the waist. |

## 4. Trolls

**What they are.** Tall, gangly, thick-skinned humanoids notorious for regenerating almost any injury within moments. They are ugly by design — warty, lumpen, long-armed, with sagging features and teeth that don't fit the mouth. The regeneration is the whole point of their look: because a troll's body heals faster than it heals *correctly*, an old troll is a catalog of everything that has ever been done to it. Limbs regrow in the wrong place, wounds seal into ridges of scar, and extra features sprout where they shouldn't. They live under bridges, in caves and in bogs, and they eat more or less anything.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Tall, stooped, disproportionately long arms reaching past the knees, small head on a thick neck, potbelly.
- **Palette:** Bog green, liver-brown, sallow gray, bruise purple. Wet, glistening highlights.
- **Material:** Rubbery hide, scar tissue, bark-hard callus, mud, rope, scavenged rags.
- **Ornamentation:** Almost none by choice — piercings that healed over, bones embedded in flesh, half-swallowed weapons still lodged in the body.
- **Escalation axis:** Regeneration made visible — wounds that never close, and the accumulated wrongness of everything healed badly.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Scrag | Gangly and warty, half-healing gashes always open somewhere on the body, mud-caked. |
| T2 | Bog Troll | Thicker limbs, visible tumorous knots of regrowth on the shoulders, a rusted blade still stuck in its side. |
| T3 | Mutant Troll | Asymmetric — an extra arm on one side, a second jaw healed into the neck, ribs grown outward like armor. |
| T4 | Scarlord | A bloated mass of overlapping regrowth, bark-hard scar plating across the chest, mismatched limbs of different sizes. |
| T5 | Elder Troll | A walking landscape of unhealed and re-healed flesh. Multiple heads at different stages of forming, weapons of a dozen armies embedded in the hide. |

## 5. Dragons

**What they are.** Enormous winged reptiles with scaled hides, long necks and tails, clawed limbs, and a horned or crested skull. They are apex predators and, in most tellings, highly intelligent — vain, territorial and possessive, hoarding treasure in mountain lairs. A dragon's power is written in its size and armament: the span of its wings, the sweep of its horns, the thickness of its scale. They are born wingless and earthbound and only grow into the sky, so a dragon's age and rank can be read directly off its silhouette.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Long neck, long tail, four limbs plus wings, wedge-shaped horned skull, deep chest.
- **Palette:** Pick one metallic or jewel tone per individual (crimson, obsidian, gold, verdigris, storm-blue), with a paler underbelly and glowing eyes.
- **Material:** Overlapping scale, horn, bone spur, leathery wing membrane.
- **Ornamentation:** Horn crowns, spinal ridges, hoard-gold fused into the hide, battle scars, tattered membrane.
- **Escalation axis:** Wingspan and horn crown — both grow dramatically at each step.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Whelp | Wingless, wet-looking scales, oversized head on a small body, nubs where horns will come in. |
| T2 | Fledgling | Small membrane wings not yet strong enough to look reliable, one clean pair of horns, bright glossy scale. |
| T3 | Drake | Full-grown. Broad wings, a proper crown of horns, metallic gleam to every scale, spinal ridge running to the tail tip. |
| T4 | Elder Wyrm | Wings that blot out the sun, membrane torn and scarred, horns thickened and chipped, gold coins fused into the belly scales. |
| T5 | Dragon Emperor | An antler-like crown of branching horns, wings like storm fronts, heat-shimmer aura, hoard-metal grown into the armor of its hide. |

## 6. Skeletons

**What they are.** Human (or humanoid) skeletons animated by magic, walking and fighting with no flesh, organs or eyes. The magic that moves them usually shows as light burning in the empty eye sockets. Individually they are the flimsiest undead — a strong hit scatters them — but they are cheap to raise and never tire, so they are fielded in enormous numbers. The stronger the necromantic power invested in one, the denser and more fused its bones become, until the top of the ladder is barely a corpse at all but a sorcerer-king wearing its own skeleton as regalia.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human skeleton proportions — narrow, angular, gaps of empty space where flesh should be.
- **Palette:** Bone white and yellowed ivory, rust, black iron, and one hot accent color for the socket-fire (green, blue or gold).
- **Material:** Bone, rusted mail, corroded steel, tattered grave-cloth.
- **Ornamentation:** Grave goods — funerary jewelry, crowns, bone spurs, orbiting bone fragments, carved runes on the skull.
- **Escalation axis:** Bone density and how much magic burns in the sockets.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Rattler | Loose, gap-toothed frame that looks about to fall apart. Dull pinpoints in the sockets, rusted shortsword. |
| T2 | Bonesoldier | Bound together in rusted mail, shield strapped to the forearm, weak ember glow in the eyes. |
| T3 | Bone Knight | Thickened, reinforced bone. Plated ribcage, helm fused to the skull, bright flame in the sockets. |
| T4 | Bone Champion | Bones fused into solid armor-like mass, horned skull, bone spurs rising off the shoulders like a crown. |
| T5 | Lich King | Skeletal but robed in gold and deep velvet, jeweled crown, staff of bound souls, halo of slowly orbiting bone fragments. |

## 7. Rotwalkers

**What they are.** Reanimated corpses — the classic zombie. They shamble, they do not speak, and they exist to spread the rot that made them. The lowest are barely holding together, sagging and slow. What makes them frightening at higher tiers is not that they decay further, but that the plague animating them becomes *more* effective: the muscles cord up, the bone reinforces, and eventually several corpses are pulled together into one larger body. They are always wet, always partly open, and always trailing the marks of whatever killed them.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human, but off — dislocated shoulders, dragging leg, head at a wrong angle, jaw hanging.
- **Palette:** Gray-green and bruise-purple flesh, dried blood brown, bile yellow, dirty linen white.
- **Material:** Rotting flesh, exposed sinew, wet bone, grave-dirt, torn burial clothes.
- **Ornamentation:** Whatever they died in — funeral clothes, work aprons, armor rusted onto the body, the weapon that killed them still embedded.
- **Escalation axis:** Decay reversing into cohesion — higher tiers are *more* physically integrated, not less.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Shambler | Sagging skin, sunken eyes, dragging one foot, grave dirt everywhere, clothing rotted to strips. |
| T2 | Ghoul | Fresher and faster. Exposed sinew along the arms, clenched jaw, hands hooked into claws. |
| T3 | Rot Brute | Muscle-corded and bloated, bone burst through the forearms into natural blades, ribcage cracked open. |
| T4 | Amalgam | Multiple corpses fused into a single hulking body — extra arms, two torsos, several heads folded into the mass. |
| T5 | Plague Lord | A cathedral of fused bodies with one central grinning face, robes of stitched shrouds, a haze of flies, throne of the dead. |

## 8. Wraiths

**What they are.** Incorporeal undead — the spirit of a dead person that stayed behind and turned hostile. A wraith has no solid body. It appears as a hooded, robed or smoke-like figure, often with nothing inside the hood but cold light where eyes should be, and its lower half typically trails away into vapor rather than ending in feet. They are cold to be near, pass through walls, and drain life by touch. The weakest are barely more than a smear of fog. The strongest have condensed into a defined, almost regal shape, closer to a monarch than a ghost.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Hooded, robed, upper body defined and lower body dissolving into trailing vapor. No visible feet.
- **Palette:** Black, charcoal, deep indigo, ash gray. One cold accent — pale blue, white or sickly green — for the eyes.
- **Material:** Smoke, tattered cloth, cold vapor, rusted chain, hollow armor.
- **Ornamentation:** Chains, funeral shrouds, spectral crowns, floating rings of frost.
- **Escalation axis:** From formless mist to a sharply defined regal silhouette.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Shade | A faint smear of vapor with two cold points of light. Barely holds a humanoid outline. |
| T2 | Haunt | A hooded shape with trailing tatters, thin skeletal suggestion of hands, dragging chain. |
| T3 | Wraith | Clearly defined robed figure, skeletal hands fully visible, spectral blade, chains orbiting the body. |
| T4 | Crowned Specter | A crowned figure whose cloak spreads outward into a black tide, frost forming on everything nearby. |
| T5 | Death Sovereign | Hollow ornate armor filled with void, scythe of frozen smoke, robe like a night sky, a court of lesser shades trailing behind. |

## 9. Vampires

**What they are.** Undead that survive by drinking blood, and who retain their intelligence, memory and appearance in death. Vampires occupy a strange double register: the newly turned are feral, filthy and rat-like, closer to an animal than a person, while the ancient ones are impossibly elegant — pale, still, beautifully dressed, and utterly inhuman behind the manners. Fangs, claws, red eyes and a total absence of color in the skin are constant. Rank in vampire society is age, and age shows as refinement.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human and tall, unnaturally still, long-fingered. Higher tiers gain a cloak or wing-cloak that widens the shape.
- **Palette:** Corpse white and bone pallor, black, deep blood red, aged gold. No warm skin tones anywhere.
- **Material:** Velvet, silk, brocade, polished leather, gold, blood-glass, bat membrane.
- **Ornamentation:** Signet rings, high collars, jeweled clasps, medals, ornate cane, family crests.
- **Escalation axis:** Bestial to aristocratic — rags become couture as the posture becomes perfect.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Thrall | Feral and hunched, filthy, rat-like teeth, bloodstained shift, clawed fingers, animal posture. |
| T2 | Bloodkin | Pale hunter in a dark coat, long claws, hair slicked back, fangs prominent, still slightly hunched. |
| T3 | Noble | Fully upright and composed. Tailored velvet, red eyes, gloves, an ornate blade or cane. |
| T4 | Ancient | An unnervingly still lord in a bat-membrane cloak, ornate rings on every finger, a face that hasn't aged in centuries. |
| T5 | Progenitor | Inhumanly beautiful, wings of pure shadow, throne of blood-glass, a crown so fine it looks grown rather than made. |

## 10. Mummies

**What they are.** Preserved corpses wrapped in linen bandages and buried with ritual and treasure, risen to walk again. The lowest are simply dried-out husks in crude wrappings, dragging themselves out of a common grave. The higher ranks were important people in life, so their preservation was expensive: gold bands sewn into the linen, jeweled collars, protective glyphs inked along every wrapping, and finally a full death-mask of beaten gold. Sand and dust follow them. The bandages never come off — they simply become more valuable.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human, stiff-limbed, arms often held forward or crossed over the chest. Wrapped from head to foot.
- **Palette:** Sun-bleached linen, sand beige, desert ocher, deep gold, lapis blue, turquoise.
- **Material:** Linen bandage, dried flesh, gold leaf, lapis, carnelian, sandstone.
- **Ornamentation:** Scarab charms, jeweled pectoral collars, glyph inscriptions, gold arm bands, headdresses.
- **Escalation axis:** The wrappings themselves become royal gold.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Husk | Crude gray bandages, one leg dragging, dried flesh showing through gaps, empty eye holes. |
| T2 | Tomb Guard | Taut and dry, bronze charms sewn into the linen, a bronze khopesh, simple headcloth. |
| T3 | Sacred Servant | Gilded bands wound over the wrappings, jeweled collar, glyphs glowing faintly along the bandages. |
| T4 | Pharaoh's Champion | Full gold death-mask, ceremonial armor over the linen, sand swirling constantly at the feet. |
| T5 | God-King | Towering headdress, gold and lapis regalia, a floating sarcophagus lid behind him like a banner, a permanent sandstorm halo. |

## 11. Demons

**What they are.** Malevolent spirits given physical form, native to a burning underworld and organized into a strict infernal hierarchy. The common visual grammar is horns, hooves or clawed digitigrade legs, red or ashen skin, bat-like wings, and fire burning somewhere inside the body. A demon's rank is legible at a glance: the more horns and the more of the body that has stopped being flesh and become live fire or obsidian, the higher it stands. The lowest are pests the size of a cat. The highest sit on thrones of magma and command legions.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Horned head, broad shoulders, digitigrade or hooved legs, long tail, bat-membrane wings.
- **Palette:** Blood red, char black, ember orange, molten yellow, obsidian. Glowing cracks throughout.
- **Material:** Charred hide, obsidian, blackened iron, molten rock, smoke, bat membrane.
- **Ornamentation:** Brands and infernal sigils burned into the skin, chains, spiked black armor, skull motifs.
- **Escalation axis:** Horn count and how much of the body is fire rather than flesh.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Imp | Cat-sized, red, pinprick horns, small bat wings, whip-thin tail, needle teeth. |
| T2 | Fiend | Human-sized, goat-legged, one pair of curling horns, ember cracks opening in the skin, brand marks. |
| T3 | Hellknight | Broad-chested, four horns, molten veins visible under the hide, black spiked plate, flaming blade. |
| T4 | Archfiend | Twice human height. Obsidian armor fused to the body, wings of black smoke, a crown of horns, chains of bound souls. |
| T5 | Demon Prince | Enthroned in magma. Horns branching into a corona above the head, body more fire than flesh, wings spanning the whole frame. |

## 12. Celestials

**What they are.** Winged beings of light and order — angels, in the broad sense. They appear as idealized humanoids in gleaming armor or white robes, with feathered wings and a ring or geometric halo above or behind the head. Their rank is expressed almost mathematically: the higher the being, the more pairs of wings it has and the more complex its halo becomes, until the face is covered entirely and the body itself is mostly light. They are beautiful but not warm — the highest ranks are closer to a mechanism than a person.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Idealized upright humanoid, symmetrical, feathered wings, halo above the head.
- **Palette:** White, ivory, pale gold, sunlight yellow, sky blue. Radiant glow.
- **Material:** Polished gold, white marble, feather, silk, pure light.
- **Ornamentation:** Halos, ring geometry, sacred script floating in the air, ribbons, censers, blank ceremonial masks.
- **Escalation axis:** Number of wings and the geometric complexity of the halo.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Messenger | Small and slight, two soft wings, plain robes, a simple ring of light, bare feet. |
| T2 | Guardian | Armored in gold plate, full feathered wings, a solid ring halo, spear and round shield. |
| T3 | Warden | Four wings. Ornate gold armor, a halo of two rotating rings, script drifting in the air around the arms. |
| T4 | Archon | Six wings, faceless masked helm, halo of interlocking geometric rings, sword of pure light. |
| T5 | Seraph | Wings beyond counting layered in tiers, body mostly light with only a suggestion of form, halo like a working orrery. |

## 13. Harpies

**What they are.** Winged humanoids with the body of a woman and the wings, legs and talons of a bird of prey. Instead of arms they have wings, or arms that end in feathered wings; their legs end in oversized raptor claws. They nest on cliffs in raucous flocks, dive on travelers, and are known for a shrieking cry. Their status is entirely plumage: a low harpy is scraggly and half-molted, while a matriarch has vivid, cared-for feathers, a raised crest, and gold rings on her ankles above the talons.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Humanoid torso, large wings replacing or extending the arms, backward-jointed bird legs with huge talons, feathered crest on the head.
- **Palette:** Slate gray, brown, dirty cream at low tiers, moving to vivid iridescent blue-green, storm purple and white at high tiers.
- **Material:** Feather, hollow bone, sinew, raw hide, gold.
- **Ornamentation:** Bone jewelry, braided hair, ankle rings, trophy feathers taken from rivals.
- **Escalation axis:** Feather quality and talon size.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Screecher | Scraggly and half-molted, patchy gray feathers, thin claws, wild matted hair. |
| T2 | Talon | Full brown plumage, hooked talons, bone bead necklaces, spread wings held wide. |
| T3 | Skyrender | Vivid iridescent feathers, a raised feathered crest, spear-length claws, wing tips banded in color. |
| T4 | Matriarch | Ceremonial plumage worn like a cloak, gold ankle rings above the talons, elaborate crest, hair braided with feathers. |
| T5 | Sky Queen | Storm-colored wings spanning cliff to cliff, crest raised into a full crown, gold cuffs on every limb, a wind halo of loose feathers. |

## 14. Minotaurs

**What they are.** Massive humanoids with the head of a bull on the body of an enormously muscled man — horned skull, bovine snout and ears, human torso and arms, and hooves or heavy clawed feet. They are traditionally associated with labyrinths, arenas and blood sport, and fight with brute-force weapons: mauls, greataxes, chained blocks of stone. A minotaur's rank shows in its horns, which spread wider and thicker with age and are often capped, banded or broken and re-forged in bronze after famous fights.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Bull head with wide horn spread on a vastly overbuilt human torso, digitigrade hooved legs, thick tail.
- **Palette:** Deep russet and black hide, bronze, ox-blood red, bone.
- **Material:** Coarse hair, horn, bronze, heavy leather harness, chain, hewn stone.
- **Ornamentation:** Nose rings, horn caps and bands, brands, arena trophies, chained slabs.
- **Escalation axis:** Horn spread and the sheer mass of the weapon carried.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Bull-kin | Young and rangy, short stubby horns, rope harness, wooden maul, no armor. |
| T2 | Charger | Thick-necked, iron-capped horns, brass nose ring, battle scars across the chest, single-bladed axe. |
| T3 | Labyrinth Warrior | Massive. Horns spreading past the shoulders, chained bronze plate, greataxe held one-handed. |
| T4 | Arena Champion | One horn broken and re-forged in bronze, trophies hung from the harness, a chained stone block as a weapon. |
| T5 | Bull Emperor | Horn arc as wide as a gateway, bronze slab armor, brazier-glow in the eyes, cape of hides, standing on a shattered arena floor. |

## 15. Nagas

**What they are.** Serpent-people: a human upper body from the waist up, and a long snake's tail instead of legs. They are typically depicted as temple-dwellers and guardians — intelligent, ancient, ceremonial, and associated with hidden treasure, sacred water and forbidden knowledge rather than mindless violence. Higher-ranking nagas gain a cobra-like hood behind the head and additional pairs of arms, and their scales are inlaid or painted with gold and gemstone patterns as a mark of rank.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human torso rising from a long coiled serpent body; hood behind the head at higher tiers; multiple arms.
- **Palette:** Emerald and jade green, deep teal, gold, bronze, ivory, with jewel-toned scale patterning.
- **Material:** Scale, gold, gemstone inlay, silk sashes, temple stone.
- **Ornamentation:** Hood crests, arm bands, gold scale-banding along the tail, jeweled brow ornaments, sacred script.
- **Escalation axis:** Coil length and the ornamentation of the hood and scales.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Serpentling | Short slim tail, plain dull scales, bare torso, blowgun or sling, no hood. |
| T2 | Coilguard | Longer coils, patterned scales, twin curved daggers, simple gold arm bands. |
| T3 | Hooded Adept | A broad cobra hood behind the head, four arms, jeweled scale inlay along the spine, silk sash. |
| T4 | Temple Guardian | Gold banding running the full length of the coil, six arms each with a weapon, ornate brow ornament, glowing sacred script. |
| T5 | Serpent Empress | Coils filling the entire frame, hood fanned into a crown studded with gems, headdress, floating relics orbiting the torso. |

## 16. Lizardfolk

**What they are.** Upright reptilian humanoids — scaled skin, clawed hands and feet, a long balancing tail, and a crocodilian or iguana-like head. They live in swamps, jungles and river deltas in tribes, using primitive but effective gear: bone spears, obsidian blades, hide shields. Unlike nagas they are tribal rather than ceremonial. Rank shows in the crest and dorsal spines along the back and skull, which grow taller and more elaborate with age, and in the thickness of the natural scale plating.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Upright reptile, forward-leaning with a long counterbalancing tail, crested head, dorsal ridge.
- **Palette:** Swamp green, mud brown, moss, ocher, with warning-bright throat and crest colors (orange, red, yellow).
- **Material:** Scale, bone, obsidian, hide, river reed, mud, feather.
- **Ornamentation:** Tribal paint, bone piercings, trophy skulls, woven reed shields, feather headdress.
- **Escalation axis:** Crest height, dorsal spines, and natural plating.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Scout | Small and lean, mud-caked, no crest, bone spear, simple hide wrap. |
| T2 | Warrior | Low crest forming, obsidian-toothed club, woven reed shield, war paint on the snout. |
| T3 | Spineback | Heavy dorsal spines down the back, thickened plate-scale across the chest, alligator jaw, twin obsidian blades. |
| T4 | Swamp Chieftain | Crocodilian bulk, trophy skulls hung on shoulder plating, feathered headdress, tail-club. |
| T5 | Saurian Tyrant | A full back-sail crest, ceremonial mantle of feathers and hide, stone tail-club, war paint across the entire body. |

## 17. Insectoids

**What they are.** Intelligent insect-people organized into a hive with rigid castes: workers, soldiers, drones, guards, and a single enormous queen. Each individual has a chitinous exoskeleton, mandibles, compound eyes, four or six limbs, and sometimes wings. They do not have individual identity in any meaningful sense — a hive acts as one organism. Caste is the entire status system, and it is visible instantly in the shape of the limbs and the polish of the shell: workers are dull and thin, soldiers are bladed, and the queen is a mountain of a body that no longer moves on its own.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Segmented body, mandibled head, compound eyes, four to six limbs, narrow waist, abdomen behind.
- **Palette:** Chitin brown and black at low tiers, moving to deep glossy crimson, iridescent green-black and amber at high tiers.
- **Material:** Chitin, resin, wax comb, translucent wing membrane, amber.
- **Ornamentation:** Ridging and fluting in the shell, resin plating, spiracle vents, comb-throne structures.
- **Escalation axis:** Caste specialization and the gloss and ornamentation of the chitin.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Worker | Dull matte brown, thin spindly limbs, small mandibles, no weapons, hunched. |
| T2 | Soldier | Hardened darker carapace, forelimbs elongated into natural blades, larger mandibles, upright stance. |
| T3 | Elite Drone | Glossy plating with ridged fluting, four arms, visible spiracle vents along the sides, wing cases. |
| T4 | Royal Guard | Ornate deeply ridged shell in crimson and gold, translucent functional wings, ceremonial spear. |
| T5 | Hive Queen | Immense segmented abdomen, crown-like head plating, tiny useless limbs, seated in a throne of wax comb, attendants at the base. |

## 18. Arachnids

**What they are.** Giant intelligent spiders and spider-people. The lower forms are simply oversized spiders — eight legs, multiple eyes, fangs, spinnerets. Higher forms take on a centaur-like arrangement: a humanoid torso rising from a spider's body. They live in webbed caverns and ruined cities, hunt by ambush, and wrap what they catch. Rank shows in the number and brightness of the eyes, the span of the legs, and the quality of the silk they wear — a broodmother's silk is worked into robes and hangings, not just webbing.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Bulbous abdomen, eight jointed legs arching above the body, clustered eyes, fangs. Humanoid torso from T3 up.
- **Palette:** Black and charcoal, deep violet, blood red markings, pale silk white, and bright eye-glow (green, red or amber).
- **Material:** Chitin, coarse bristle, silk, bone, amber.
- **Ornamentation:** Silk robes and drapery, egg sacs, wrapped prey used as decoration, jeweled eye-settings.
- **Escalation axis:** Eye count, leg span, and how finely the silk is worked.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Crawler | Dog-sized, dull brown fur, six simple eyes, thin legs, dripping fangs. |
| T2 | Stalker | Barbed legs, venom-slick fangs, bright patterning on the abdomen, first web decorations. |
| T3 | Weaver | Spider-centaur — humanoid torso on the spider body, eight bright eyes, silk-wrapped blades in each hand. |
| T4 | Broodmother | Swollen egg-sac abdomen, robes of woven silk, jeweled settings around each eye, hundreds of tiny spiderlings on the body. |
| T5 | Weaver Queen | Legs like cathedral arches, a throne of layered web, a crown of clustered glowing eyes, silk drapery filling the space behind her. |

## 19. Slimes

**What they are.** Amorphous blobs of translucent, corrosive jelly with no bones, face or fixed shape. A slime moves by flowing, eats by engulfing, and digests whatever it engulfs — so anything it has swallowed and not yet dissolved is visible suspended inside its body. Somewhere in the mass is a core, usually a denser glowing nucleus, which is both its brain and its weak point. As slimes grow they gain internal structure: first a defined core, then a crust, then something disturbingly close to a body held together by nothing.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Rounded, drooping, gravity-obeying blob; upper tiers approximate a humanoid outline without ever hardening into one.
- **Palette:** Translucent blue, acid green, amber, or violet — pick one per individual — with a brighter core color.
- **Material:** Viscous jelly, surface tension sheen, suspended debris, crystalline core.
- **Ornamentation:** Whatever is trapped inside — coins, bones, armor, weapons — plus surface crust and drip trails.
- **Escalation axis:** Internal structure emerging out of formlessness.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Droplet | Fist-sized, almost clear, a single dark dot for a core, one wobbling pseudopod. |
| T2 | Blob | Knee-high, cloudy, swallowed coins and small bones drifting inside, a slow drip trail behind it. |
| T3 | Ooze Knight | Roughly humanoid shape, a glowing crystalline core in the chest, a hardened crust across the shoulders like armor. |
| T4 | Devourer | Towering, with a full skeleton suspended and posed inside the mass, absorbed armor plates floating in place. |
| T5 | Primordial Ooze | A near-liquid titan with a dozen cores, absorbed weapons and armor arranged into the suggestion of a face, a tide of lesser slimes at its base. |

## 20. Golems

**What they are.** Constructed servants — bodies built out of inanimate material and animated by magic, with no life of their own and no free will. A golem has no face in the usual sense, only a carved mask or a blank front, and the magic that runs it shows as glowing runes or seams of light along the joints. They are made, not born, so quality of workmanship *is* rank: a village conjurer makes a lumpy clay figure, while a master artificer produces something of marble, gold and gemstone that looks more like a monument than a machine.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Blocky, heavy, thick-limbed humanoid with oversized hands, small or absent head, visible joint gaps.
- **Palette:** Clay red-brown, gray stone, granite, black basalt, white marble and gold at the top tiers. Rune-glow in blue, gold or orange.
- **Material:** Clay, fieldstone, iron banding, carved granite, basalt, marble, gold inlay, gemstone.
- **Ornamentation:** Carved runes, glowing seams, iron bindings, sculptural relief, a gemstone heart.
- **Escalation axis:** Material quality — clay, to stone, to marble and gemstone.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Clay Figure | Crude and lumpy, uneven limbs, fingerprints still in the surface, a single rune scratched on the forehead. |
| T2 | Stone Servant | Fieldstone body held together by iron bands, faint glowing seams at the joints, blank slab face. |
| T3 | Rune Warrior | Carved granite, properly proportioned, engraved runes covering the whole torso, stone fists like anvils. |
| T4 | Basalt Colossus | Huge black basalt body, plates floating slightly apart and held by magic, molten core visible through the chest cavity. |
| T5 | Master Golem | White marble with gold inlay, sculptural relief carved across every surface, a gemstone heart, a rune-halo hovering above the shoulders. |

## 21. Elementals

**What they are.** Magical entities whose temporary bodies are shaped from neutral spell-matter or
one of the four base affinities: Fire, Earth, Wind, or Water. An Elemental has no flesh, fixed face,
ordinary clothing, or handheld weapon. One binding seed or core holds its body together. Its outer
shape remains recognizable when its affinity changes: an Earth form is not a Fire creature covered
in rocks, and a Water form is not a flame recolored blue. The substance itself becomes the body.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** A small asymmetric teardrop at T1; from T2 onward, a legless hovering upper body with exactly two arm channels and a tapering lower body. A flame-like contour describes shape, not Fire.
- **Palette:** Normal is desaturated silver-gray. Fire is vermilion, Earth ocher-brown and umber, Wind pale green-gray, and Water deep blue.
- **Material:** Neutral spell-matter, living combustion, circulating earth magic, compressed air, or cohesive water according to affinity. Never treat affinity as a decorative aura over another body.
- **Ornamentation:** One seed, then an exposed core, a divided ring, three mantle sections, and finally four core facets with matching orbital vanes.
- **Escalation axis:** Form coherence, core complexity, and control over shaped affinity substance.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Wisp | Palm-sized asymmetric teardrop or flame-like entity with a pointed crest, continuous tapering tail, one visible seed, no face, and no limbs. |
| T2 | Elemental Spirit | Human-sized faceless hovering torso with exactly two tapering arms, one central core, and a legless lower body dissolving into a continuous tail. |
| T3 | Elemental Warden | Fully coherent hovering upper body with exactly two strong arm conduits, a divided ring core, broad shoulders, and a narrow tapering lower body. |
| T4 | Elemental Colossus | Large floating upper body with exactly two integrated arms, three articulated mantle sections, and a shuttered central core. |
| T5 | Elemental Sovereign | Tall complete hovering figure with exactly two coherent arms, a four-facet central core, four orbital vanes, and an affinity-shaped arena wake. |

For the Wisp, the flame-like outline is shared shape language. Fire makes it living vermilion
combustion; Earth makes it an ocher-brown and umber flow of soil, clay, silt, and fine mineral grains;
Wind makes it pale green-gray compressed air; Water makes it deep-blue cohesive liquid; and Normal
makes it desaturated silver-gray neutral spell-matter. Earth settles and compacts rather than burns,
and must not show red flame, embers, smoke, char, lava, or a solid rock-projectile body.

## 22. Treants

**What they are.** Trees that walk. A treant has a trunk for a body, roots for legs, branches for arms, and a face suggested in the bark — knots for eyes, a split for a mouth. They are slow, immensely strong, and immensely old; the eldest have been standing in one place for centuries and carry entire ecosystems on their bodies. Age is the only status they recognize, and it is written in girth, canopy size, and how much other life has taken up residence in them.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Vertical trunk-body, root-cluster legs, long branching arms, canopy spreading above the head.
- **Palette:** Bark brown and gray, deep moss green, autumn gold, and a warm amber glow at the heartwood.
- **Material:** Bark, heartwood, moss, lichen, sap, vine, stone caught in the roots.
- **Ornamentation:** Nesting birds, hanging vines, mushrooms, carved runes in the bark, growth rings visible at old wounds.
- **Escalation axis:** Age, girth, and how much other life is growing on the body.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Sapling-walker | Thin and whip-like, few leaves, shallow root feet, barely a face in the bark. |
| T2 | Young Treant | Thicker trunk, rough bark, moss across the shoulders, a proper canopy of leaves. |
| T3 | Oakheart | Broad oak-form, heavy root legs, dense foliage, vines hanging from the arms, clear bark face. |
| T4 | Elder Warden | Ancient and hollow-trunked, with an amber glow visible in the heartwood cavity, birds nesting in the branches, mushrooms along the base. |
| T5 | World-Tree Warden | A walking forest — a waterfall running down its back, boulders caught in the roots, a canopy that fills the sky, entire groves growing on its shoulders. |

## 23. Fungalfolk

**What they are.** Humanoid mushrooms. A fungalfolk has a stalk for a body, a cap for a head, gills underneath, and a network of mycelium threading through its pale, spongy flesh. They live in caves, rot and deep forest, communicate through spores rather than speech, and glow softly in the dark. They spread by releasing spores, so the more powerful ones are surrounded by a permanent visible cloud. Rank is cap size and luminosity — a sovereign's cap is a structure in its own right.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Cap-headed, stalk-bodied, slightly bulbous limbs, wider at the top than the base.
- **Palette:** Pale bone-white flesh, cap in one strong tone (crimson, violet, ocher or teal), bioluminescent cyan or green at the gills.
- **Material:** Fungal flesh, spongy cap tissue, mycelium threads, damp wood, moss, spore dust.
- **Ornamentation:** Spore clouds, glowing gill patterns, smaller mushrooms growing off the body, mycelium veins under translucent skin.
- **Escalation axis:** Cap size, bioluminescence, and spore density in the surrounding air.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Sporeling | Knee-high, plain brown cap, stubby limbs, no glow, faint puff of dust when it moves. |
| T2 | Mycon | Human-sized, spotted cap, faint cyan glow at the gills, a club of hardened stalk. |
| T3 | Sporeguard | Wide luminous cap, mycelium veins visible under translucent skin, a constant thin spore cloud around the shoulders. |
| T4 | Fungal Shaman | Cap spread like a parasol, glowing script written in suspended spores in the air, smaller mushrooms growing along the arms. |
| T5 | Mycelial Sovereign | A cap the width of a courtyard, a drifting spore aurora overhead, a throne of interlocked mushrooms, glowing mycelium spreading across the ground. |

## 24. Deep Ones

**What they are.** Fish-people from the ocean floor. They have humanoid bodies with fish or amphibian features — bulging lidless eyes, gill slits at the neck, webbed and clawed hands, scaled or slick eel-like skin, and a wide lipless mouth of needle teeth. They worship things in the deep and come ashore in raiding parties. The deeper and older a deep one is, the less human it looks: pressure-adapted bulk, tentacular growths, and anglerfish-style bioluminescent lures growing from the head.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Humanoid but hunched and long-armed, finned forearms and spine, wide flat head, hunched gill-neck.
- **Palette:** Drowned green, deep-sea blue-black, sickly gray, pearlescent belly white, with cold cyan or green bioluminescence.
- **Material:** Wet scale, eel skin, coral, barnacle, driftwood, rusted metal, whale bone.
- **Ornamentation:** Coral shard armor, barnacle crusting, fishing net, pearl and shell jewelry, glowing lures.
- **Escalation axis:** Loss of the humanoid look, and how much of the body produces light.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Fishman | Hunched and gasping, dull gray scales, visible gills, a rusted hook or gutting knife. |
| T2 | Tidewarrior | Finned forearms and calves, coral-shard armor over the chest, webbed claws, a barbed spear. |
| T3 | Abyssal Hunter | Broad and eel-skinned, a small glowing lure on the brow, a trident, barnacles crusting the shoulders. |
| T4 | Deep Priest | Tentacular beard and growths from the jaw, pressure-scarred plating, glowing organs visible through translucent skin, a staff of whale bone. |
| T5 | Leviathan Lord | Kraken-limbed and whale-scaled, colossal, a crown of anglerfish lures, robes of net and kelp, a court of lesser deep ones below. |

## 25. Werebeasts

**What they are.** Humans cursed to transform into an animal — wolf, bear, boar, tiger — or, more usefully for a monster roster, humans frozen partway through that transformation. A werebeast is a hybrid: a human frame stretched onto animal proportions, with a muzzle, thick fur, digitigrade legs, claws and fangs, still wearing the shredded remains of human clothing. Their power is measured by how far into the beast they have gone and how permanently they are locked there.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human frame with animal proportions — hunched shoulders, elongated muzzle, backward-jointed legs, long arms, tail.
- **Palette:** Gray, black, russet or silver fur, with yellow or moon-white eye glow, torn cloth in muted human colors.
- **Material:** Fur, hide, torn clothing, broken chain, iron collar, claw and fang.
- **Ornamentation:** Shredded remnants of human dress, snapped restraints, ritual scars, moon-motif jewelry from the human life.
- **Escalation axis:** How far into the beast form the body is locked, and how little of the human remains.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Afflicted | Mostly human. Patchy fur, wolfish yellow eyes, elongated fingers, clothing intact but torn at the seams. |
| T2 | Half-turned | Crouched hybrid, elongated snout, fur across the back and arms, clothing hanging in strips. |
| T3 | Werebeast | Full anthropomorphic beast, thick pelt, digitigrade legs, heavy claws, only a belt and rag left of the human. |
| T4 | Alpha | Scarred silver pelt, oversized fangs, moon-glow in the eyes, snapped chain still hanging from an iron collar. |
| T5 | Primal Beast-King | Dire-scaled bulk, a mane like a storm, a crown of horns or antlers grown from the skull, shattered binding-runes floating around the body. |

## 26. Gargoyles

**What they are.** Stone statues that come alive — the grotesque figures carved onto cathedral roofs and city walls, which climb down at night. A gargoyle has a squat, crouching build, bat-like wings, clawed hands and feet, horns, and a snarling beast face, all rendered as carved stone rather than flesh. When dormant it is indistinguishable from architecture. Rank shows in the quality of the stone it was carved from and the sophistication of its wings, which move from crude bat flaps to full architectural spans.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Squat and crouching, hunched shoulders, folded wings above the back, clawed grip, horned head.
- **Palette:** Sandstone tan, granite gray, weathered green-black, marble white and verdigris bronze at the top. Rune-glow in amber.
- **Material:** Carved stone, sandstone, granite, marble, bronze, mortar, moss and water-staining.
- **Ornamentation:** Architectural carving, relief detail, water-spout mouths, glowing seams, pedestal fragments still attached.
- **Escalation axis:** Stone quality and the architecture of the wings.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Grotesque | Small and perched, chipped sandstone, crude features, wings too small to be useful. |
| T2 | Sentinel | Crouched, granite skin, weathered and moss-streaked, small bat wings, blunt claws. |
| T3 | Guardian | Standing upright, carved relief detail across the body, broad wings, glowing amber seams at the joints. |
| T4 | Cathedral Warden | Marble and verdigris bronze, spired horns, ornate carved armor, wings framed like window tracery. |
| T5 | Stone Sovereign | Architectural in scale, wings shaped like flying buttresses, a throne carved from its own pedestal, statuary attendants at the base. |

## 27. Djinn

**What they are.** Powerful spirits of air and fire, bound into lamps, rings and bottles, and famous for granting wishes on terms that favor the djinn. Visually they are a humanoid upper body — arms, torso, head, often muscular and richly dressed — whose lower half never resolves into legs, instead trailing away into a column of smoke. They wear gold cuffs and jewelry that mark their binding. Rank is how much of the body has abandoned solid form entirely.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Solid humanoid torso and arms above, tapering to a smoke plume below. Always hovering, never standing.
- **Palette:** Deep sapphire or violet smoke, brass and gold, turquoise, with a warm inner glow.
- **Material:** Smoke, vapor, gold, brass, silk sash, gemstone.
- **Ornamentation:** Gold arm cuffs and shackles, floating rings, jeweled headpiece, bound lamps orbiting the body, sacred script in the smoke.
- **Escalation axis:** How much of the body is smoke rather than solid form, and the scale of the whole.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Wisp-spirit | Small, mostly vapor, only a half-formed face, faint glow, no jewelry. |
| T2 | Lesser Djinn | Defined humanoid torso trailing into smoke, plain gold wrist cuffs, bare chest, sash. |
| T3 | Djinn | Sharply defined figure, ornate sashes and jewelry, arms of swirling visible wind, gold rings orbiting the forearms. |
| T4 | Great Djinn | Giant scale, storm-colored smoke body, jeweled crown, sacred script written in the vapor around it. |
| T5 | Wish-Sultan | Palace-scale. A vortex for a lower body, a floating throne, a constellation of bound lamps orbiting, robes woven from smoke and gold. |

## 28. Fae

**What they are.** Spirits of the wild wood — fairies, in the older and less friendly sense. Small ones are palm-sized winged sprites trailing glowing dust. Larger ones are human-sized and beautiful in a way that is subtly wrong: proportions slightly off, faces a little too still, eyes without whites. They wear living plants rather than fabric, grow antlers of woven branches, and their wings run from simple insect membrane to elaborate stained-glass structures. The higher the fae, the more alien and the less comprehensible the beauty.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Slender, elongated, weightless-looking, with layered wings behind and branching growths from the head.
- **Palette:** Spring green, petal pink, dusk violet, moth-wing iridescence, gold pollen light.
- **Material:** Living leaf and petal, spider silk, stained glass, bark, dew, moth wing.
- **Ornamentation:** Glowing pollen and motes, crowns of woven branch, antlers, flower garlands, masks.
- **Escalation axis:** Wing complexity, and how strange the beauty becomes.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Pixie | Palm-sized, simple insect wings, trailing glowing dust, a thorn for a sword. |
| T2 | Sprite | Knee-high, layered petal wings, garland clothing, thorn blade, faint aura of motes. |
| T3 | Fae Knight | Human-sized, stained-glass wings, antlers of woven branches, armor of leaf and bark, an unnervingly still face. |
| T4 | Fae Noble | Gown of living leaves and flowers, a mask-like face with no whites in the eyes, a hovering court of motes, crown of thorns and blossom. |
| T5 | Fae Monarch | Impossible geometry in the limbs and wings, a crown that cycles through all four seasons at once, wings like a shattered rainbow, reality bending faintly around the silhouette. |

## 29. Void Horrors

**What they are.** Creatures from outside the world — entities that do not obey the rules the rest of reality follows. They are not monsters with more limbs and bigger teeth; they are shapes that the eye cannot quite finish resolving. Where every other race gains detail as it rises in power, void horrors *lose* it: the highest ranks are less physically present, not more, until a sovereign is essentially a hole in the world shaped roughly like a being, ringed with eyes.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Roughly humanoid or many-limbed, but with edges that don't close and proportions that shift.
- **Palette:** Matte void black, negative space, deep purple-black, with star-flecks and one cold accent (violet, white or sick green) for the eyes.
- **Material:** Absence, matte non-reflective black, star-field interior, fractured geometry.
- **Ornamentation:** Rings of unblinking eyes, orbiting geometric shards, non-Euclidean folds, no jewelry of any kind.
- **Escalation axis:** Subtraction — coherence is lost as power rises.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Crawler | A low, scuttling thing of too many thin limbs, matte black, no face, no reflection. |
| T2 | Hollow | A humanoid outline with a smooth hole where the face should be, edges slightly blurred. |
| T3 | Fracture | A body that folds the wrong way, clusters of eyes orbiting where the head should be, star-flecked interior visible through gaps. |
| T4 | Encroacher | A vast shape only partly present in the scene, edges dissolving into negative space, limbs that end before they end. |
| T5 | Outer Sovereign | A silhouette-shaped absence in the world, ringed with unblinking eyes, geometry that refuses to resolve no matter how long you look. |

## 30. Doppelgangers

**What they are.** Shapeshifters that copy people. In their true form they are blank gray humanoids — smooth, featureless, no hair, no face, no distinguishing marks at all, like an unpainted mannequin. They take the shape of others to infiltrate, and the quality of the copy is their entire measure of power. Weak ones produce obviously flawed imitations. Strong ones are indistinguishable from the original. The very strongest stop bothering to be one person at a time and wear many stolen faces at once.

**Visual constants**
- **Silhouette:** Human, generic, deliberately unremarkable. Higher tiers add layered faces and mask motifs.
- **Palette:** Blank clay gray and putty, with borrowed colors from stolen forms bleeding in at the edges.
- **Material:** Smooth featureless flesh, clay-like skin, mask porcelain, borrowed cloth.
- **Ornamentation:** Seams, wrong joints, stolen clothing that doesn't quite fit, masks worn in layers.
- **Escalation axis:** How convincing the disguise is, and what leaks through it.

| Tier | Name | Visual |
|------|------|--------|
| T1 | Blank | Smooth gray humanoid, no face, no hair, no clothing. Unfinished-looking. |
| T2 | Halfling Copy | A half-copied form — one arm still unformed gray, a face that is nearly right but blurred at the mouth. |
| T3 | Mimic | A near-perfect human copy, betrayed only by faint seams at the jaw and one hand jointed the wrong way. |
| T4 | Facewearer | A flawless copy wearing three faces at once, layered like masks, each shifting to the front in turn. |
| T5 | Faceless Sovereign | A shifting parade of stolen faces moving across the entire body — chest, arms, back — under a crown made of masks. |

## Creature taxonomy

“Monster race” is useful conversationally but too ambiguous for mechanics, lore, and image
production. The wiki separates several axes so an artist does not turn a combat job into anatomy and
an implementer does not turn ancestry into behavior.

## Classification axes

| Axis | Question | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | What kind of existence produced it? | natural, constructed, undead, elemental, summoned, interdimensional |
| Visual/content family | Which creatures share a production grammar? | goblinoids, constructs, beasts, draconic |
| Species | Which physiology and life cycle are shared? | goblin, gray wolf, cave drake |
| People/race | Is there a sapient population with identity/culture? | only when authored; never inferred from combat labels |
| Faction | Which organization or allegiance acts here? | tower warband, bound order, wild pack |
| Duty/archetype | What battlefield or civic job is performed? | skirmisher, archer, guard, healer, builder |
| Variant | Which bounded individual/content variation applies? | regional, evolved, injured, equipped, affinity expression |

A “goblinoid family plate” may contain several species or one people with several duties; the image
alone cannot decide. A “Tower Summoner” is an archetype, not a species. “Undead” is usually an origin
or state, not automatically a people.

## Personhood and hostility

Sapience, personhood, hostility, and faction are separate fields.

- A sapient species is not biologically evil because tower content deploys hostile members.
- A non-sapient predator can be dangerous without moral intent.
- A construct may have no agency, bounded agency, or full personhood depending on its authored
  cognition—not its material.
- Undead status does not answer whether memories, autonomy, or rights remain.
- Encounter content must name the duty, coercion, faction, hunger, territorial behavior, or other
  cause that produces hostility.

This distinction matters mechanically: conversation, surrender, negotiation, morale, capture,
knowledge, social consequences, and synthesis ethics depend on what kind of being is present.

## Mechanical inheritance

Definitions inherit only through explicit references:

```text
origin definition
  + species physiology
  + individual attribute/affinity profile
  + faction knowledge/equipment
  + duty policy and skills
  + encounter-local state
  = deployed actor
```

Family or species may constrain anatomy and typical affinity ranges. It may not assign intelligence,
morality, personality, worth, or combat role without an explicit authored reason. Individual
variation remains possible within content rules.

## Wiki page selection

Create a page when a classification has enough stable content to brief another worker:

- family page: shared visual grammar and production catalog;
- species page: physiology, life cycle, affinity distribution, senses, and variants;
- people/race page: culture, social organization, history, language, internal variation;
- faction page: goals, membership, resources, duties, and relationships;
- archetype page: combat/civic role, policy, tells, skills, counters;
- individual page: unique identity and canonical history.

Until the distinction is known, keep a draft family page and list the taxonomy question explicitly.

## Goblinoid warbands

Design status: **draft family page built from accepted first-wave encounter content**

## Player-facing bestiary entry

Goblinoid warbands are the tower's first coordinated opponents. Their danger comes from readable
roles, communication, formation, and escalating cooperation rather than from an assumption that
every member is physically overwhelming.

Skirmishers teach guard timing. Archers create ranged lanes. Hexweavers create interruptible control.
Chieftains rally formations and force threat-priority decisions. Together they introduce the player
to the idea that a functioning enemy system is more dangerous than four unrelated stat blocks.

## Implemented combat grammar

| Archetype | Role | Skills/tells | First teaching goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goblin Skirmisher | mobile melee pressure | knife fighting, feinting, pack movement | guard timing and target focus |
| Goblin Archer | ranged lane control | shortbow, repositioning, marking | cover and closing distance |
| Goblin Hexweaver | telegraphed magical control | ember hex, binding mark, concentration | ward reading and interrupts |
| Goblin Chieftain | formation leader | command, heavy cleave, rally | formation disruption and threat priority |

These descriptions are bound to `packages/content/src/enemy-roster.ts`. The full anatomy, culture,
life cycle, language, and noncombat behavior are not yet canon.

## Affinity profile

No family-wide affinity profile is accepted. “Ember hex” suggests that the Hexweaver may use a
[Fire](../../mechanics/affinities/fire.md)-related skill or effect, but a skill name is not evidence
that all goblinoids possess Fire affinity. Future entries must separate individual profile, learned
skill, equipment, faction practice, and species distribution.

## Canonical family/form database

The [canonical monster database](../monsters/README.md) is the generated review surface for all 38
families and 190 forms. It publishes family classification caveats, visual constants, escalation,
combat duties, teaching goals, telegraphs, counterplay, lower-form interactions, and the complete
950-coordinate production plan.

The accepted [fauna family expansion](../fauna-family-expansion.md) adds eight separately produced
families and 40 designed forms. Its 200 coordinates are owner-approved and wired into the wiki
runtime through separate approval and runtime catalogs; it does not reopen or regenerate the
original 750.

All family floor slots remain unassigned. This database does not invent tower order, numerical
difficulty, region, release grouping, or level scaling.

## Production status and evidence

For the original 30-family database, the five production coordinates began as ownership plans.
Normal begins as

generation while human review remains pending; it is neither approval nor runtime promotion.

experiment remains immutable candidate evidence, while its exact 200 accepted images are promoted
through separate approved-source and wiki-runtime layers.

## Required entry depth

Before a family can drive broad image generation, its wiki work should answer:

1. What classification is actually shared?
2. Which anatomy, materials, scale, senses, locomotion, and life-cycle facts are stable?
3. Is it sapient or a people? If so, what internal variation and non-hostile life exist?
4. Why do encountered members fight?
5. Which attributes, affinities, skills, resistances, and vulnerabilities are typical?
6. Which roles and variants appear across tower bands?
7. What must remain visually recognizable across anatomy, equipment, injury, and evolution?
8. Which facts are public, discovered through play, or internal spoilers?
9. Which asset views are required: taxonomy plate, identity anchor, combat scale, states, motion?
10. Which content IDs, policies, tests, and runtime assets prove implementation?

Use the [monster family template](../../templates/monster-race-template.md).

## Expansion workflow

Future family additions enter as source packets first, pass taxonomy and five-form normalization,
and receive a separate manifest revision. They do not enter the canonical count merely by adding an
image or an occupational class. That order prevents visual production from freezing unclear biology
or mechanics.
