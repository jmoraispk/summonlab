## Visual constants

- Humanlike proportions scaled upward with a heavy brow, thick limbs, dense bone, and a slight stoop that communicates the body's own weight.
- Stone gray, granite blue-gray, earth brown, moss green, glacier white, and warm amber eyes remain the family palette without requiring an elemental origin.
- Rough hide, raw stone, moss, lichen, pine, iron chain, and rope accumulate as bodily geology and oversized practical tools.
- Chains, standing-stone jewelry, skin runes, and whole trees function as ornament at a scale that stays legible from the arena.
- Raw scale and geological coverage escalate together until the Sovereign carries a valley, but every form retains recognizable humanoid load paths and slow committed motion.

| T1 — Initiate | [Brute](../giant-brute.md) | Falling-Timber Sweeper (Initiate) | Clear a broad but slow front with a tree-trunk club, leaving the fallen timber as a temporary obstacle that changes one simple route through the arena. | Teach the Giant family's readable mass: watch the heels and hands to identify a committed arc, leave it early, then use the displaced landscape during the long recovery. | Timber Fall: the Brute grips its tree trunk at one end and drops the full length across a marked lane; the club remains grounded until the Giant walks its hands down and lifts again, creating cover on one side and an exposed hand on the other. | Its weathered hands slide to the trunk's far end, the rear heel digs a dark crescent in the soil, and the bare shoulder rises beneath the weight. | Cross behind the planted heel before the trunk falls, then strike the grounded hand or use the fallen timber to block the Brute's shortest turn. | Not applicable for this form. |
| T2 — Soldier | [Hill Giant](../giant-hill-giant.md) | Slope-Roll Pressure Soldier (Soldier) | Apply repeatable ranged pressure by hauling and releasing rope-cradled boulders down selected lanes, then occupying the new low ground behind them. | Teach that Giant terrain attacks have visible load paths: the rope belt, shoulder moss, and first back plates show where the boulder is tied and which slope will carry it. | Hill Roll: the Giant unhooks one boulder from its rope belt, braces it against a forming back plate, and releases it along the lower of two visible slopes; the empty rope loop then drags behind and can be stepped on during retrieval. | Moss sheds from one shoulder, the corresponding rope loop pulls taut, and the Giant tests the ground with a broad foot before turning the boulder downhill. | Move to higher ground or behind the dropped tree cover, then pin or cut the empty dragging loop before the Giant retrieves another boulder. | A Brute can knock a stalled boulder into a new line with Timber Fall, but the same trunk can wedge the roll if the player draws the two attacks together. |
| T3 — Specialist | [Stone Giant](../giant-stone-giant.md) | Chain-Megalith Orbit Controller (Specialist) | Replace simple linear sweeps with a moving circular exclusion zone, using a chained megalith to divide the arena and pull displaced terrain into a deliberate orbit. | Teach the qualitative Specialist pivot: read chain geometry, move through the quiet center rather than away from raw size, and punish the fixed pivot when the megalith changes orbit. | Megalith Orbit: the Stone Giant anchors the chain around a boulder-ridged forearm and walks a measured circle, swinging the megalith through a wide outer ring while the space beside its cliff-face brow remains calm. Reversing direction requires the Giant to plant the megalith, step across the chain, and expose the anchor wrist. | Iron chain rises link by gate-sized link, the megalith scrapes a bright circular track, and runes along one forearm align toward the intended orbit before the first swing. | Enter the calm inner radius, cross behind the planted megalith during reversal, and strike or bind the exposed wrist anchor before the chain lifts into the opposite orbit. | Hill Giants roll boulders into the outer orbit and Brutes sweep them loose, expanding the ring only while the Stone Giant maintains its anchor; breaking the wrist pivot returns those attacks to independent lanes. |
| T4 — Champion | [Mountain Lord](../giant-mountain-lord.md) | Watershed Route Commander (Champion) | Control multiple terrain routes by opening shoulder waterfalls, dropping pine-marked scree, and assigning lower Giants to exploit the dry route that remains. | Teach players to read a living landscape as a command interface: water, pines, and ring-sized chains announce which route will flood, collapse, or remain safe before the Mountain Lord commits. | Three Watersheds: the Mountain Lord turns one gate-sized iron ring at its waist to open a shoulder fall, tilts a pine-crag to release scree across a second route, and points lower forms toward the third. | T5 — Sovereign | [Primordial Titan](../giant-primordial-titan.md) | Marching-Valley Worldboss (Sovereign) | Serve as the changing arena itself, carrying a valley that tilts through timber, slope, orbit, and watershed phases while lower Giants operate visible anchors across its back. | Test mastery of every scale-reading lesson by asking players to identify the Titan's next weight shift, disable the lower-form crew shaping that phase, and move between stable landscape anchors before the valley changes orientation. | March of the Range: the battle occupies the valley on the Titan's back. At four gait beats, the Titan repeats the family grammar at landscape scale: a forested ridge falls like Timber Fall, a glacier rolls loose like Hill Roll, a chain of standing stones sweeps around a central peak like Megalith Orbit, and shoulder rivers reroute through Three Watersheds. A Brute, Hill Giant, Stone Giant, or Mountain Lord operates the matching rune anchor; disabling that crew and breaking its anchor removes the phase. After two anchors fall, the Titan kneels and rotates the valley ninety degrees, opening new paths through its beard glaciers rather than becoming a larger version of the Champion. | Clouds shear at the waist, furnace-warm eyes narrow without casting an aura, one valley rune darkens, and the corresponding forest, glacier, standing-stone chain, or river system begins moving several beats before the Titan's foot lands. | Use the announced landscape route to reach the active lower Giant, defeat or displace it, fracture the exposed rune anchor during the Titan's footfall recovery, and relocate to a surviving standing-stone shelter before the valley rotates; every destroyed anchor permanently removes one gait phase. | Each lower Giant operates the anchor that enlarges its learned mechanic, and the Titan's phase ends early when that operator is removed; surviving operators relocate when the valley rotates, preserving readable continuity across the boss encounter. |
