## Visual constants

- Tall, broad, top-heavy humanoid silhouette with a thick neck, wide shoulders, heavy jaw, forward lean, and lower tusks rising past the lip.
- Grey-green or ash-grey skin is framed by blood red, black, dark iron, bone white, and readable red or white war paint.
- Boiled leather, fur, riveted iron, bone, and crude steel remain thick, weighty, and practical rather than elegant or finely matched.
- Scars, paint, tusk rings, piercings, skull trophies, spiked pauldrons, and clan totems communicate earned encounter rank without becoming a biological claim.
- Tusk length, muscle mass, posture, and carried trophies escalate together, but every form retains committed heavy motion and exposed recovery as the family combat grammar.

| T1 — Initiate | [Grunt](../orc-grunt.md) | Committed Shoulder-Tester (Initiate) | Probe the front line with a short spear or hand-axe rush that displaces a guarded target but leaves the lean attacker stranded if the rush misses. | Teach the Orc family's basic bargain: broad shoulders and heavy intent make the approach dangerous, while planted feet and a forward lean make the recovery readable and punishable. | Shoulder Test: the Grunt lowers one hide-wrapped shoulder behind its crude weapon and drives straight through the first occupied space; contact shoves both combatants, while a miss carries the Grunt past the target before it can turn. | It scrapes one bare foot backward, tucks its small blunt tusks, and draws the spear or axe tight beside the lowered shoulder. | Step across the narrow line rather than retreating, then strike the unarmored ribs or back while the Grunt plants its forward foot and turns. | Not applicable for this form. |
| T2 — Soldier | [Warrior](../orc-warrior.md) | Warpaint Breach Soldier (Soldier) | Maintain reliable line pressure by using an iron-banded shoulder check to open a guard, then occupying the breach with a controlled heavy-axe follow-through. | Teach players to read paint and stance as a two-beat sequence: the red eye-band signals the advancing shoulder, while the first scars and axe angle reveal which side will be exposed afterward. | Red-Line Breach: the Warrior smears fresh paint down one axe cheek, checks forward behind iron-banded leather, then sweeps toward the painted side; the second beat occurs only if the shoulder makes contact. | It drags two fingers through the red band across its eyes, marks one axe cheek, and turns the matching shoulder forward. | Dodge to the unpainted side or stop the shoulder before contact; either response cancels the sweep and exposes the Warrior's underarm while the heavy axe resets. | A Warrior can stop beside an overextended Grunt and use the Grunt's spear line as the edge of its breach, but separating their lanes prevents the paired shove-and-sweep. |
| T3 — Specialist | [Berserker](../orc-berserker.md) | Scar-Rhythm Pursuer (Specialist) | Turn a stable front into a moving tempo hazard by chaining alternating twin-cleaver lanes and forcing the player to interrupt cadence rather than merely retreat from a tougher attacker. | Teach the qualitative Specialist pivot: the Berserker's threat comes from an authored left-right rhythm encoded in its scar lattice and pauldrons, and breaking that rhythm is safer than outrunning it. | Scar-Lattice Bloodbeat: each cleaver strike follows the next diagonal of the chest scar lattice, alternating sides and widening the pursuit arc; completing three beats triggers a crossed finishing chop, but a clean interruption or a miss resets the sequence and leaves both arms spread. | One spiked pauldron rolls forward, the matching cleaver taps a bright scar intersection, and the chest expands on a clearly countable beat before each diagonal advance. | Cross behind the pauldron that just moved, force one beat to miss against open space or terrain, or interrupt on the visible scar tap; the reset exposes the unplated scar lattice before the cleavers can recross. | Grunts and Warriors try to hold targets inside the next marked diagonal, but their bodies also block the Berserker's widening route and can be used to force a sequence-breaking miss. |
| T4 — Champion | [Warchief](../orc-warchief.md) | Skull-Totem Line Commander (Champion) | Plant and rotate a clan front around a skull totem, ordering committed attacks into complementary lanes while guarding the command point with paired greataxes. | Teach commander prioritization: lower Orcs lose their coordinated recoveries when the totem falls, but the Warchief's fixed two-axe answer makes a frontal assault on it a deliberate trap. | Totem Turn: the Warchief rotates the skull totem toward one arena quarter, causing Grunts to shoulder-test inward, Warriors to breach across them, and Berserkers to begin Bloodbeat along the painted boundary; the Warchief then locks both greataxes into a slow crossing guard that cannot pivot away from that quarter. | The gate-sized iron rings on its tusks strike together, the skull totem tips toward a painted quarter, and every nearby Orc turns its marked shoulder or scar lattice along the same boundary. | Move against the indicated rotation, bait the fixed crossing guard into empty space, then cut the totem lashings from the rear; without it, lower forms resume local tells and no longer cover one another's recoveries. | The totem assigns lanes to Grunts, Warriors, and Berserkers and shortens their exposed resets only while they face its selected quarter; its loss breaks the formation into independent attacks. |
| T5 — Sovereign | [Overlord](../orc-overlord.md) | Trial-of-Standards Tyrant (Sovereign) | Command a phased arena trial in which captured standards define challenge lanes, surviving lower forms earn transformations into the next duty, and the Overlord personally enforces whichever standard remains standing. | Test mastery of commitment, breach sides, Bloodbeat rhythm, and totem rotation by making players choose which visible standard to disable before its trial promotes the Orc formation. | Trial of Standards: the Overlord plants three enemy standards around a central ribcage throne and impales one with its banner-pole to begin a trial. The Rush standard sends Grunts through narrow shoulder lanes and promotes a survivor into a Warrior; the Red standard pairs Warriors into crossing breaches and marks a survivor as a Berserker; the Skull standard lets a Warchief rotate every remaining lane. Breaking a standard permanently removes that trial. After each break, the Overlord tears one rib plate from its great-beast armor to wield as a curved arena gate, changing the remaining routes rather than simply gaining power. | The dagger-long tusks hook a chosen standard, the cloak of hides swings toward its painted lane, and the matching lower forms strike weapons or scars in unison while one rib plate visibly unlocks from the armor. | Read the selected standard, defeat or displace its designated survivor before promotion, attack the standard during the Overlord's committed enforcement sweep, and use each removed rib gate to block the next trial lane; destroying all standards ends promotions and forces a direct final duel with permanently opened armor seams. | Grunts, Warriors, Berserkers, and any Warchief receive standard-specific routes and promotion duties; players can deny those transformations, and every destroyed standard permanently removes one category of lower-form command. |
