## Visual constants

- Every form retains a four-legged ursine body, plantigrade paws, compact tail, broad muzzle, dense coat, and weight-bearing shoulder hump.
- Escalation uses coat condition, scars, claw specialization, posture, and environmental interaction rather than humanoid equipment.
- Raised shoulders, planted hind legs, open chest, and exposed inner wrists preserve readable commitment and recovery windows.

## T1–T5 combat progression

| T1 — Initiate | [Scrub Bear](../bear-scrub-bear.md) | Swipe-Commit Initiate | Deliver one broad forepaw swipe after standing, then drop heavily onto all fours. | Teach the family's basic promise that enormous commitment produces a generous recovery window. | Standing Swipe: the Bear rises, loads one shoulder, and sweeps once before an unavoidable heavy landing. | Its hips settle and the striking shoulder rises above the other forepaw. | Move under the raised side and punish the landing recovery from the rear quarter. | Not applicable for this form. |
| T2 — Soldier | [Cave Bear](../bear-cave-bear.md) | Maul-Line Soldier | Advance behind crossed forelegs, absorb frontal pressure, then open into a two-paw maul. | Teach players to disengage from guarded frontal mass and seek a rear-quarter angle. | Crossed Maul: protected advance ends when both forearms open into a fixed two-swipe sequence. | The forearms cross, claws scrape, and its chest expands before opening. | Attack from the rear quarter or disengage until the second swipe exhausts and exposes the chest. | Scrub Bears pressure the exposed side while the Cave Bear's crossed advance fixes attention. |
| T3 — Specialist | [Hookclaw](../bear-hookclaw.md) | Grapple Specialist | Hook a limb, shield, or obstacle and drag it sideways to break defensive formations. | Introduce an asymmetric specialist whose dangerous side and planted recovery side must be read separately. | Hook and Haul: one enlarged curved claw catches the target while the opposite hind foot becomes a visible towing anchor. | The hooked forepaw turns palm-up and the opposite hind foot digs deeply into the ground. | Dodge toward the planted foot or strike the exposed inner wrist before the sideways drag begins. | Cave Bears block the obvious retreat while Scrub Bears harry targets displaced by the drag. |
| T4 — Champion | [Scarback](../bear-scarback.md) | Terrain-Breaker Champion | Bring down a tree, pillar, or boulder as moving cover before charging through the debris. | Teach players to read the environment as part of the Bear's commitment and escape before the route closes. | Falling Territory: Scarback tests one structure, topples it into a fixed lane, then follows through the debris. | Its broad back scars stretch while both forepaws test the chosen structure. | Leave the fall lane or attack the planted hind legs before the structure gives way. | Lower Bears use the fallen structure as flank cover, but lose that advantage if the collapse is interrupted. |
| T5 — Sovereign | [Ancient Ursine](../bear-ancient-ursine.md) | Den-Territory Sovereign | Claim three den landmarks and alternate roar, upheaval, and maul phases while commanding lesser Bears around the active landmark. | Test committed-swipe windows, guarded fronts, grapple anchors, and terrain collapse as one territorial encounter. | Three Den Marks: the Ancient rubs one shoulder against a landmark to empower the matching lower-form behavior until that mark is broken. | The marked shoulder sheds fur and pigment onto one landmark before the corresponding phase begins. | Break the active mark or draw the pack to an unclaimed zone, then punish the Ancient's exposed rubbed shoulder. | Scrub Bears guard the roar landmark, Cave Bears hold the maul landmark, Hookclaw displaces intruders, and Scarback reshapes the route to the active mark. |
