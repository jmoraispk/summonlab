A natural feline expedition family centered on concealment, facing, isolation, explosive pounces, and hunting-territory control.

## Family visual grammar

- Every form retains a four-legged feline body, flexible spine, retractile claws, whiskered muzzle, mobile ears, and expressive tail.
- Species variation remains feline and is communicated by coat pattern, limb proportion, mane, canine length, and hunting posture rather than equipment.
- Eye line, tail stillness, shoulder compression, cover disturbance, and landing posture preserve readable attack direction.

**Escalation axis:** A simple cover pounce becomes high-ground hunting, sightline isolation, saber-tooth pinning, and finally an apex territory hunt that assigns every lower form a role.

**This form's visual delta:** Apex lion-like cat with asymmetric mane, long athletic body, scarred nose, and crown-shaped ear silhouette formed by natural tufts.

## Combat reading

### Battlefield duty

Divide the arena into stalking, pursuit, sightline, and pinning territories before leading a final crossing hunt.

### Teaching goal

Test cover reading, vertical facing, eye-line control, and pin recovery in a staged apex hunt.

### Signature mechanic

Four Hunting Grounds: the Sovereign traces a territory edge and assigns one lower Cat's rule to it before personally crossing the last open boundary.

### Telegraph

Its asymmetric mane and tail trace the next boundary while the assigned lower Cat takes a visible position.

### Counterplay

Disrupt the assigned Cat to open that boundary, then move through it before the Sovereign crosses.

### Lower-form interaction

Wildcat defines stalking cover, Lynx defines high ground, Panther defines the sightline, and Sabercat defines the pin territory; removing one permanently opens its ground.
