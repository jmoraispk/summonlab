Source-draft Mummies are treated as ritually preserved, linen-wrapped dead whose grave goods and offices supply authored encounter ranks, not as a biological people or evidence about any real culture, religion, ethnicity, divinity, royal legitimacy, morality, or afterlife. Sand and dust are family-native burial residue and non-affinity staging; true affinities require separate material and behavior construction.

## Family visual grammar

- A stiff-limbed human or humanoid silhouette remains wrapped from head to foot, with arms held forward, crossed, guarding, inscribing, or commanding through readable linen tension.
- Sun-bleached linen, sand beige, desert ochre, deep gold, lapis blue, and turquoise remain funerary materials and symbols rather than affinity colors.
- Linen bandage, dried flesh, gold leaf, lapis, carnelian, bronze, and sandstone grave goods stay materially distinct and retain visible knots, stitches, glyph routes, and breakable seals.
- Family-native sand and dust follow disturbed wrappings, tomb movement, and burial objects without becoming Earth or Wind affinity by themselves.
- Escalation adds precious thread, glyph density, collars, masks, armor, headdresses, and sarcophagus regalia while the bandages never come off and always explain binding and counterplay.

**Escalation axis:** Crude wrapping knots become bronze guard bindings, processional glyph circuits, a masked tomb formation, and a royal sarcophagus engine whose dismantled cartouches remove commands and unwrap a simpler final phase.

**This form's visual delta:** A fully wrapped royal dead figure in towering gold-and-lapis headdress and regalia, framed by a sarcophagus lid divided into four cartouche quarters and a permanent sandstorm halo of family-native, non-affinity burial sand rather than Earth or Wind affinity; the halo stays bounded to the royal silhouette and readable rails.

## Combat reading

### Battlefield duty

Command a royal tomb boss through a floating sarcophagus lid and four cartouche rails that bind, guard, rewind, and form lower Mummies before each broken rail simplifies the arena and final wrapped body.

### Teaching goal

Test mastery of loose knots, loaded charm stitches, glyph order, and mask formations while teaching that the source title does not imply invulnerability: every royal command depends on a breakable material seal.

### Signature mechanic

Royal Passage: the sarcophagus lid floats behind the God-King like a banner on four linen-and-gold rails. The Knot cartouche pulls Husks from common-grave alcoves; the Bronze cartouche equips survivors as Tomb Guards; the Script cartouche lets a Sacred Servant record a three-point procession; and the Mask cartouche closes a Pharaoh's Champion formation. The towering headdress points to one cartouche while family-native sand follows the moving rail, never acting independently. Cutting the lit cartouche's linen hinge permanently drops that lid quarter and removes its command. After two quarters fall, the God-King steps out of the remaining frame; its headdress shortens with each lost rail and it cycles only surviving rites around exposed wrapping knots.

### Telegraph

One lapis headdress vane aligns with a cartouche, its gold glyph becomes faintly readable, the matching lid rail swings forward, and lower Mummies cross their arms in the selected learned posture.

### Counterplay

Read the named cartouche and lower-form tell, disrupt the required Mummy or route, then cut the exposed linen hinge while the rail is forward; every dropped lid quarter removes one rite, clears an arena lane, and exposes another royal wrapping knot.

### Lower-form interaction

The four cartouches produce or command Husks, Tomb Guards, Sacred Servants, and a Pharaoh's Champion; each severed hinge permanently ends that lower-form rite and simplifies the God-King's remaining phase.
