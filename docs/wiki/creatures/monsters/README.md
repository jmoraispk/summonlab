# Canonical monster family database

Wiki ID: `creatures.monster-database`

This database publishes **30 production families**, **150 canonical T1–T5 forms**, and **750
Normal/Fire/Earth/Wind/Water image coordinates**. Family is a production and encounter grouping;
it does not by itself establish biology, ancestry, personhood, culture, morality, or universal
hostility. Read the [creature taxonomy](../taxonomy.md) before turning a family label into lore.

No floor order or difficulty value is assigned here. Every `floorSlot` remains unassigned until a
separate authored combat integration chooses it.

Production statuses describe owned attempts, not finished images. Candidate, approved-review, and
runtime are separate states; this export promotes nothing.

## 01 · Goblins

| Family field | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Family ID | `goblins` |
| Floor assignment | Unassigned |
| Classification caveat | Source-draft Goblins are treated as a visual and encounter-production family, not as an accepted universal taxonomy. The packet preserves their short, wiry scavenger identity and violent loot hierarchy while expressing combat behavior as readable encounter roles. |
| Escalation axis | Scrap becomes treasure, and posture straightens from a cringing crouch to an upright swagger; rising forms turn stolen equipment into increasingly organized mob control, battlefield infrastructure, and finally a royal loot-production system. |
| Source | [Generator bestiary](../monster-races/generator-bestiary-01.md) |

**Visual constants**

- Short, hunched, long-armed humanoid silhouette with an oversized head and pointed ears, disproportionately large hands and feet, hooked nose, and crowded crooked teeth.
- Sickly green or grey-green skin against muddy browns, rust-orange, dull iron, and sparse accents of stolen gold.
- Every carried object reads as stolen, scavenged, or crudely repaired: rags, patched leather, cracked wood, rusted scrap metal, bone, and rope rather than purpose-built matching equipment.
- Loot is the status language: mismatched helmets, coin necklaces, teeth, rings, captured banners, and trophies accumulate with rank.
- Posture and finish communicate hierarchy, from a knock-kneed cringing crouch to an upright swagger amid increasingly prestigious plunder.

## 02 · Orcs

| Family field | Definition |

**Visual constants**

- Tall, broad, top-heavy humanoid silhouette with a thick neck, wide shoulders, heavy jaw, forward lean, and lower tusks rising past the lip.
- Grey-green or ash-grey skin is framed by blood red, black, dark iron, bone white, and readable red or white war paint.
- Boiled leather, fur, riveted iron, bone, and crude steel remain thick, weighty, and practical rather than elegant or finely matched.
- Scars, paint, tusk rings, piercings, skull trophies, spiked pauldrons, and clan totems communicate earned encounter rank without becoming a biological claim.
- Tusk length, muscle mass, posture, and carried trophies escalate together, but every form retains committed heavy motion and exposed recovery as the family combat grammar.

- Chains, standing-stone jewelry, skin runes, and whole trees function as ornament at a scale that stays legible from the arena.
- Raw scale and geological coverage escalate together until the Sovereign carries a valley, but every form retains recognizable humanoid load paths and slow committed motion.

- Healed-over piercings, embedded bones, lodged weapons, misplaced limbs, stray jaws, and layered scars record prior injury as readable construction history.
- Escalation adds accumulated misregrowth rather than simple size: open gashes become knots, alternate organs, defensive scar architecture, and finally a walking archive of incompatible healing.
