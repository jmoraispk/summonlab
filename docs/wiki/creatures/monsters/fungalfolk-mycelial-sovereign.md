Source-draft Fungalfolk are treated as a mushroom-bodied visual and encounter-production family. Claims about habitat, communication, society, cognition, agency, personhood, reproduction, rank, and universal behavior are proposals pending taxonomy review. Bioluminescence, spores, mycelium, and damp surroundings are native fungal motifs rather than affinities.

## Family visual grammar

- Cap-headed, stalk-bodied silhouette with gills, bulbous limbs, and a wider top than base remains recognizable.
- Pale fungal flesh and one strong cap tone contrast with bounded cyan or green gill bioluminescence from T2 onward.
- Fungal flesh, spongy cap, mycelium, damp moss, and spore dust remain organic and spatially coherent.
- Cap breadth, gill light, spore density, secondary growth, and ground mycelium escalate at explicit source tiers.

**Escalation axis:** Cap reach and network control progress from a dust puff to battlefield spore writing and finally a sovereign throne connected to the arena floor.

**This form's visual delta:** Courtyard-wide cap, drifting spore aurora, throne of interlocked mushrooms, and luminous mycelium across the ground.

## Combat reading

### Battlefield duty

Command a throne-linked ground network that produces lower-form fruiting bodies at four readable nodes.

### Teaching goal

Test cap tilt, gill cadence, cloud direction, and spore script while each solved node permanently severs arena control.

### Signature mechanic

Arena Production — Four Fruiting Nodes: the courtyard-wide cap lights one ground-mycelium route from the interlocked mushroom throne, producing the announced lower form; solving its lesson exposes that node beneath the drifting spore aurora.

### Telegraph

The aurora narrows over one network route, one throne mushroom opens, and the linked lower-form silhouette fruits at its endpoint.

### Counterplay

Resolve the announced lower-form rule, then sever the exposed mycelial node; every cut collapses one throne section and prevents that production pattern.

### Lower-form interaction

Sporeling, Mycon, Sporeguard, and Fungal Shaman each fruit from one node; its learned counterplay exposes the node for permanent removal.
