## Per-image component audit

### `cp2-ref-01` — Party hierarchy

- **Silhouette:** The foreground figure, tall central mantle, compact scout, and broad guardian make
  the party hierarchy readable despite overlap.
- **Body and height:** Relative height is visible, but most bodies remain long-legged and narrow.
- **Face and hair:** Four strong hair masses carry recognition at a glance.
- **Costume and material:** Large dark shapes, one accent per person, and restrained metal are
  useful; some cuts read more modern than Pack 2 should.
- **Pose and personality:** Forward placement, chin angle, and shoulder set communicate leadership,
  caution, calm, and protection without text.
- **Status cue:** Placement and visual contrast communicate importance more effectively than glow.
- **Adopt:** party-level silhouette planning, one dominant identity cue, posture-led hierarchy.
- **Avoid:** treating the slim dark-haired swordsman as the universal protagonist template.

### `cp2-ref-02` — Ornate card grammar

- **Silhouette:** Figure poses remain distinct inside a rigid shared card format.
- **Body and height:** Cropping and uniform cards suppress meaningful scale comparison.
- **Face and hair:** Hair color and hood shapes separate identities efficiently.
- **Costume and material:** Gold filigree and black geometry create a premium collectible object.
- **Pose and personality:** Varied torso direction prevents the set from feeling completely cloned.
- **Status cue:** Star row and frame density are explicit but overwhelm character-specific rarity.
- **Adopt:** controlled frame geometry, a shared collectible grammar, clear identity-safe cropping.
- **Avoid:** generic gold as the only power signal, identical star treatment at every tier.

### `cp2-ref-03` — Cropped cast comparison

- **Silhouette:** Hair, collar, and shoulder shapes carry most recognition at this crop.
- **Body and height:** The crop provides almost no trustworthy body or height evidence.
- **Face and hair:** Age lines, facial hair, hair mass, and one non-human luminous form create useful
  portrait-level separation.
- **Costume and material:** Red uniform, dark tailoring, and cool armor form clear material groups.
- **Pose and personality:** Expression range is modest but readable.
- **Status cue:** Luminous anatomy and armor complexity suggest power without requiring a label.
- **Adopt:** age lines, grooming, collar silhouettes, material-based status.
- **Avoid:** using portrait diversity as proof that the full-body cohort is diverse.

### `cp2-ref-04` — Mixed-role roster segment

- **Silhouette:** Helm, shaggy coat, gold shoulder armor, hood, and school uniform read as roles.
- **Body and height:** Card crops again conceal scale; most visible bodies remain slender.
- **Face and hair:** Hair and eyewear diversify faces, though several youthful faces converge.
- **Costume and material:** Strong comparison between armor, ordinary clothing, and occult costume.
- **Pose and personality:** Small gestures help, especially eyewear adjustment and open expressions.
- **Status cue:** Armor density and exposed skin compete as attention devices.
- **Adopt:** occupation-coded clothing, mixed armor coverage, readable hats and hoods.
- **Avoid:** corsetry, cleavage, or exposure as the primary feminine identity or power cue.

### `cp2-ref-05` — Rank and physique contrast

- **Silhouette:** Hooded swordsman, formal fighter, horned armored figure, suited operative, and
  streetwear characters are immediately distinct.
- **Body and height:** One very muscular adult expands the male range, while feminine forms remain
  comparatively narrow and idealized.
- **Face and hair:** Age, beard, eyewear, and vivid hair diversify the roster.
- **Costume and material:** Useful contrast between plain cloth, tailoring, ceremonial armor, and
  extravagant high-rank fashion.
- **Pose and personality:** Shoulder angle and expression communicate aggression or composure.
- **Status cue:** High-rank characters receive both stronger motifs and more rendering emphasis.
- **Adopt:** rank-specific motif density, older adults, muscular variation, formal/non-combat roles.
- **Avoid:** unequal physique range by presentation, exposed torso as shorthand for strength.

### `cp2-ref-06` — Age and occupation range

- **Silhouette:** Suits, armored shoulders, open collar, capes, and utilitarian layers create role
  separation even in repeated card shapes.
- **Body and height:** The visible cohort includes older, broader, and heavier-set men but gives
  feminine characters less equivalent build variety.
- **Face and hair:** This is the strongest source for wrinkles, facial hair, eyewear, and adult age
  separation.
- **Costume and material:** Tailoring mixed with armor demonstrates that combat readiness need not
  mean full plate.
- **Pose and personality:** Formal and open bearings are readable through shoulders and expression.
- **Status cue:** Shoulder treatment and material finish carry rank more than color alone.
- **Adopt:** older summons, grooming range, broad/soft adult bodies, profession-first tailoring.
- **Avoid:** reserving age and mass diversity mostly for masculine characters.

### `cp2-ref-07` — Whole-roster audit

- **Silhouette:** The complete sheet exposes useful class variety but also repeated narrow torsos,
  similar head scales, and similar card poses.
- **Body and height:** True height cannot be compared, and the majority share one idealized build.
- **Face and hair:** Hair color and accessories do more work than facial construction.
- **Costume and material:** Fantasy armor, uniforms, streetwear, and occult clothing create breadth.
- **Pose and personality:** Repeated portrait framing limits posture expression.
- **Status cue:** Some high-status figures are obvious, but no consistent tier grammar spans all
  characters.
- **Adopt:** audit at cohort scale; mix combat, institutional, and civilian role signals.
- **Avoid:** declaring diversity from many outfits when anatomy and proportions still repeat.

### `cp2-ref-08` — Ensemble marketing composition

- **Silhouette:** Overlapping half-bodies create energy and a clear central protagonist.
- **Body and height:** Perspective and cropping make height claims unreliable.
- **Face and hair:** Six distinct hair and face shapes survive the busy background.
- **Costume and material:** Modern outerwear, armor, formal clothing, and bright accents demonstrate
  how a later multi-style pack could remain cohesive through rendering rather than era.
- **Pose and personality:** Gesture and lean create a lively party rather than a police lineup.
- **Status cue:** Central scale and lighting dominate over explicit rarity marks.
- **Adopt:** marketing-only ensemble staging, controlled overlap, material contrast.
- **Avoid:** using a marketing composition as an identity or scale reference.

### `cp2-ref-09` — Full-body decorated knight

- **Silhouette:** Long coat tails, asymmetric shoulder ornament, sword angle, and fitted leg armor
  form a strong knight identity.
- **Body and height:** Full-body framing helps, but the anatomy remains an idealized long-legged
  narrow-waist template.
- **Face and hair:** The compact pale hair mass contrasts well with the red torso.
- **Costume and material:** Red cloth, white trousers, steel, and restrained gold create excellent
  material separation and hierarchy.
- **Pose and personality:** The relaxed sword pose reads as practiced rather than frantic.
- **Status cue:** Masterwork construction and motif repetition make the character prestigious.
- **Adopt:** motif repetition, asymmetric ornament, practical equipment contact, material hierarchy.
- **Avoid:** heeled armored footwear, narrowed armor waist, or fashion anatomy that weakens stance.

### `cp2-ref-10` — Feminine proportion comparison

- **Silhouette:** Hair length, ear shape, boots, and clothing provide three different outlines.
- **Body and height:** All three share extremely long legs, narrow waists, similar height, and little
  mass variation—the clearest negative reference for Pack 2.
- **Face and hair:** Hair variation is stronger than face or body variation.
- **Costume and material:** Everyday clothing beside fantasy armor is useful for later packs, but
  the middle design uses exposure and a high-cut costume as its dominant cue.
- **Pose and personality:** Neutral presentation supports comparison, though one glamour pose
  undermines anatomical review.
- **Status cue:** Fantasy armor reads as more powerful, but exposure is incorrectly mixed into the
  signal.
- **Adopt:** neutral full-body comparison sheets, visible hair and footwear differences.
- **Avoid:** uniform feminine anatomy, glamour posing, exposure-led differentiation.

## Derived Pack 2 principles

1. **Audit silhouettes before portraits.** Every six-person cohort must remain distinct in a flat
   dark silhouette at roster size.
2. **Show true scale.** Height and proportion reviews use a common floor line, camera, focal length,
   and head scale; cropped cards cannot validate body diversity.
3. **Cross body and presentation intentionally.** Feminine, masculine, and androgynous cohorts each
   include short, tall, wiry, soft, athletic, sturdy, and powerful examples.
4. **Let role change construction.** Armor coverage, cloth weight, tailoring, tools, and stance
   communicate occupation before decorative symbols do.
5. **Use material hierarchy for power.** Craftsmanship, rare material behavior, integration, and
   resonance geometry communicate star evolution; gold and glow remain restrained.
6. **Make low-rank art equally premium.** A `1★` hero can own simple equipment without receiving
   weaker anatomy, lighting, composition, texture, or identity specificity.
7. **Preserve attractiveness without objectification.** Style, fitness, elegance, and charisma may
   vary, but exposure and anatomy exaggeration never carry rarity or human value.
8. **Separate review roles.** Identity anchors prove the person, lineups prove scale, continuity
   sheets prove persistence, and marketing compositions prove group energy.
9. **Keep the medieval pack materially coherent.** Pack 2 uses practical cloth, leather, mail,
   plate, wood, bone, stone, and restrained arcane materials. Later packs may change era and culture.
10. **Treat evolution as authored continuity.** Star evolution may change physique, style, and
    resonance anatomy only through versioned state data bound to the same recognizable identity.
