# Fauna Family Expansion Design

Status: **owner-approved for family design**

Date: 2026-09-04

## Purpose

Add a strong expedition-first fauna layer without reopening or regenerating the completed original
monster image matrix. This design adds nine families, each with five authored combat forms and the
same Normal, Fire, Earth, Wind, and Water visual axes used by the existing enemy program.

## Non-regeneration boundary

The existing `family-affinity-v1` program remains frozen at 30 families, 150 forms, and 750
coordinates. Its targets, assignments, candidates, review evidence, approvals, and runtime state are
not inputs to a rerun. This expansion creates a separate future queue containing only:

```text
9 new fauna families × 5 new forms × 5 image targets = 225 new coordinates
```

The number 975 may describe the combined design catalog, but it is **not** a generation instruction.
No existing-family image is regenerated by this design. No image is generated, approved, or
promoted to runtime in this batch.

## Family grammar

Fauna families use the established five-tier grammar while remaining recognizably animal rather
than becoming armored humanoids:

| Tier | Function | Fauna expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 — Initiate | First readable lesson | Ordinary or lightly exaggerated animal; one simple tell and punish window |
| T2 — Soldier | Reliable pressure | Mature baseline predator or herd threat that repeats the family lesson |
| T3 — Specialist | Qualitative pivot | Distinct anatomy or behavior changes positioning, targeting, terrain, or attack cadence |
| T4 — Champion | Elite combination | Scarred, adapted, or dominant animal combining earlier lessons without becoming a boss recolor |
| T5 — Sovereign | Family boss | Apex animal transforms the encounter and actively uses lower forms or their established rules |

Tiers describe encounter complexity and visual form, not numerical level. Expeditions may scale a
lower form above an earlier higher form without changing its identity.

## 31 · Wolves

Wolves teach pack geometry. Their escalation moves from opportunistic flanking to coordinated prey
designation and an apex pack hunt.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Grey Wolf | Flank-Probe Initiate | Circles toward an exposed side, then commits to one tripping bite when the target turns away. | Shoulders drop and the outside forepaw crosses inward; face it or step across the approach, then punish the missed bite. | Lean ordinary wolf, grey coat, long legs, intact ears, no equipment. |
| T2 | Dire Wolf | Hamstring Pursuer | Alternates pursuit with a packmate; its low rear-leg bite briefly slows isolated prey. | Head flattens beside the shoulder and the partner widens formation; break their angle or stagger the committed biter. | Heavier chest and jaws, darker coarse coat, old flank scars, still unmistakably lupine. |
| T3 | Howler | Pack-Tempo Specialist | A directional command howl advances allies on one side while wolves on the opposite side hold, changing the pack's attack rhythm. | Throat ruff expands and the muzzle points toward the advancing wing; interrupt the inhale or cross through the temporarily stationary wing. | Tall narrow-chested wolf with a pale throat ruff, resonant chest cavity expressed physically, and no magical instrument. |
| T4 | Pack Alpha | Prey-Marking Champion | Fixes one target with a scent mark, intercepts attacks aimed at lesser wolves, and swaps the active pursuit wing after every lunge. | Tail locks level, nose paints a visible scent trail, and one foreleg braces; cleanse distance across terrain or bait the intercept away from the pack. | Broad mature wolf with torn ear, neck mantle, dominant upright posture, and accumulated scars rather than armor. |
| T5 | Greatwolf | Hunt-Circle Sovereign | Establishes a tightening hunt circle with four readable breach points, calling Grey Wolves to probe, Dire Wolves to pin, the Howler to change tempo, and the Alpha to close the weakest exit. | Hackles rise in sequence around the circle before the Greatwolf commits to one breach; defeat or displace the assigned lower form and escape through its opening. | Monumental but anatomical wolf, deep mane, long scarred muzzle, distinctive broken upper canine, and arena-command posture. |

## 32 · Bears

Bears teach commitment, mass, and recovery windows. Their escalation moves from readable swipes to
grappling, terrain displacement, and den-scale control.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Scrub Bear | Swipe-Commit Initiate | Delivers one broad forepaw swipe after standing, then drops heavily onto all fours. | Hips settle and the striking shoulder rises; move under the raised side and punish the landing recovery. | Small shaggy bear, long muzzle, uneven seasonal coat, ordinary claws. |
| T2 | Cave Bear | Maul-Line Soldier | Advances behind crossed forelegs, absorbs frontal pressure, then opens into a two-paw maul. | Forearms cross and claws scrape before the chest opens; attack from the rear quarter or disengage until the maul exhausts. | Massive cave-adapted bear with high shoulders, dense dark coat, worn claws, and pale muzzle scars. |
| T3 | Hookclaw | Grapple Specialist | Hooks a shield, limb, or obstacle with one enlarged curved claw and drags it sideways, breaking defensive formations. | The hooked forepaw turns palm-up and the opposite hind foot digs in; dodge toward the planted foot or strike the exposed inner wrist. | Asymmetric bear with one visibly enlarged hook claw, thickened wrist, and a lower pulling stance. |
| T4 | Scarback | Terrain-Breaker Champion | Rears against a tree, pillar, or boulder and brings it down as moving cover before charging through the debris. | Back scars stretch and both paws test the chosen structure; leave its fall lane or attack the hind legs before the structure gives way. | Huge scarred bear with splintered debris caught in its coat and a recognizable pale scar across the back. |
| T5 | Ancient Ursine | Den-Territory Sovereign | Claims three den landmarks, alternating roar, upheaval, and maul phases; lesser Bears become more aggressive only near the currently claimed landmark. | The Ancient rubs its marked shoulder against one landmark before that phase; break the mark or draw the pack to an unclaimed zone. | Old broad-skulled apex bear with silvered mantle, broken claws, landmark pigments in its fur, and immense grounded weight. |

## 33 · Boars

Boars teach lane prediction, bracing, and displacement. Their escalation changes a simple charge into
terrain-cutting routes and a sovereign stampede.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Bristle Hog | Straight-Line Initiate | Scrapes once, then makes a short straight charge that cannot turn. | One forehoof paws twice and the head lowers between the shoulders; sidestep and punish the exposed flank. | Compact wild hog with raised bristles, short tusks, and a low ordinary body. |
| T2 | Tunnel Boar | Brace-and-Charge Soldier | Braces inside narrow ground, then drives targets out of cover with a longer committed charge. | Both hind hooves settle and dust funnels backward; leave the lane or make it collide with solid terrain. | Thick-necked cave boar with worn forehead, longer tusks, mud-packed coat, and reinforced shoulder callus. |
| T3 | Razorback | Furrow-Cutting Specialist | Cuts a curved furrow with one tusk; its next charge follows that route and throws debris outward. | The inside tusk stays buried while the back bristles ripple along the future curve; cross the furrow before the charge or strike the rooted outside leg. | Tall sail-like bristle ridge, asymmetric plow tusk, and visibly overbuilt shoulder on the cutting side. |
| T4 | War Tusker | Formation-Breaker Champion | Hooks barriers and rotates them into new lanes, then signals lesser Boars to charge through the opening. | Neck twists beneath the hooked object and the free tusk points down the new lane; release or destroy the object, or stagger the planted rear leg. | Towering scarred boar with splayed paired tusks, chipped forehead shield, and heavy neck folds. |
| T5 | Old Tusk | Stampede Sovereign | Creates three successive stampede lanes, assigning Bristle Hogs, Tunnel Boars, and Razorbacks to them before personally collapsing the remaining safe lane. | Each tusk scores one lane and the mane rises from tail to skull before the final collapse; interrupt a lane leader to preserve its safe gap. | Enormous ancient boar with one broken outer tusk, root-tangled mane, layered shoulder scars, and a low unstoppable silhouette. |

## 34 · Burrowers

Burrowers are one segmented subterranean lineage. They teach ground reading, eruption timing, exposed
rear-segment counterplay, and arena reshaping rather than functioning as a miscellaneous cave bin.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Soil Grub | Ridge-Trace Initiate | Travels just below the surface as a short moving ridge and emerges for one bite. | Three evenly spaced soil pulses lead the head; step perpendicular and strike the soft body during re-entry. | Pale compact segmented grub, small digging mandibles, soft rear plates. |
| T2 | Burrowing Worm | Eruption Soldier | Crosses a longer underground lane, erupts beneath a target, and leaves its rear segments above ground while turning. | A widening ridge accelerates and pebbles jump at the endpoint; avoid the endpoint and attack the stranded rear segments. | Long annelid body, armored front rings, softer flexible rear, broad shovel mandibles. |
| T3 | Hookjaw Burrower | Drag-Under Specialist | Opens two hooked jaws around terrain or a target and retracts toward its tunnel, changing horizontal spacing into a rescue check. | Paired ground cracks converge and both jaw tips briefly surface; break either convergence line or strike the jaw hinge before retraction. | Split hook mandibles, flattened head shield, muscular collar rings, and a narrower towing body. |
| T4 | Tremor Worm | False-Ridge Champion | Produces several decoy ridges while its heavy body travels beneath only one, then erupts in a sweeping body roll. | False ridges shed loose dust while the real ridge displaces embedded stones; follow the stone movement and punish the exposed tail after the roll. | Thick armored middle rings, vibration feelers, scarred dorsal plates, and a hammer-shaped terminal tail. |
| T5 | Deep Maw | Tunnel-Network Sovereign | Opens a network of persistent tunnel mouths, routes lower Burrowers through them, and collapses one sector each phase before exposing its feeding collar. | Tunnel lips contract in sequence toward the chosen collapse and the collar plates flare; block a feeder tunnel, evacuate the sequence, then strike the open collar. | Vast ringed burrower with petaled feeding maw, layered front armor, many tactile whiskers, and a vulnerable pale collar behind the head. |

## 35 · Great Cats

Great Cats teach concealment, facing, isolation, and explosive commitment. Their escalation remains
feline while moving from ordinary stalking to pride-level hunting control.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Wildcat | Brush-Pounce Initiate | Breaks line of sight once, then pounces from the nearest low cover. | Tail tip stops and the cover edge parts; face the opening or step toward it to force an early short pounce. | Lean tawny wildcat, long tail, tufted ears, light spotting. |
| T2 | Lynx | High-Ground Soldier | Bounds between raised perches and attacks the target showing the longest exposed back. | Ear tufts flatten and one perch sheds dust; rotate toward the perch or vacate its landing cone. | Larger compact cat with long hind legs, short tail, facial ruff, and oversized paws. |
| T3 | Panther | Sightline Specialist | Moves through connected shadow or foliage lanes and isolates one target by threatening any ally who crosses its marked sightline. | Shoulder rosettes disappear sequentially into cover while the eyes remain fixed; flood the lane with movement or attack the still-visible eye line. | Long low black cat with faint rosettes, reflective eyes, flexible shoulders, and an uninterrupted predatory silhouette. |
| T4 | Sabercat | Pinning Champion | Commits both long canines to a ground pin; lesser cats gain safer approach angles until it withdraws them. | Jaws open fully and forepaws widen before the leap; dodge inside the leap and punish the planted canines or exposed ribs. | Heavy forequarters, paired saber canines, short powerful tail, and scarred shoulders. |
| T5 | Pride Sovereign | Hunting-Territory Sovereign | Divides the arena into stalking, pursuit, and pinning territories, assigning Wildcat, Lynx, Panther, and Sabercat behaviors before leading a final crossing hunt. | Mane and tail trace the next territory boundary while the matching lower cat takes position; disrupt that cat to open the boundary before the Sovereign crosses. | Apex lion-like cat with asymmetric mane, long athletic body, scarred nose, and a crown-shaped ear silhouette formed by natural tufts. |

## 36 · Serpents

Serpents teach strike range, body-state reading, venom management, and coils as terrain. They remain
limbless snakes and do not overlap the humanoid Naga family.

| Tier | Form | Archetype | Battlefield duty and mechanic | Telegraph and counterplay | Visual progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Adder | Range-Probe Initiate | Coils once and strikes exactly one body length. | Neck forms a tight S and the tongue stops flicking; step beyond the fixed reach and punish the extended neck. | Small thick-bodied viper, zigzag markings, short fangs. |
| T2 | Constrictor | Space-Hold Soldier | Loops around one obstacle or target and tightens only while its head stays anchored. | Tail completes a visible circle and the jaw clamps the anchor point; break the loop or stagger the head. | Long muscular snake with broad gripping belly scales and blunt wedge head. |
| T3 | Spitting Cobra | Venom-Lane Specialist | Fans its hood and paints two visible venom lanes, forcing movement before a conventional strike through the remaining gap. | Hood spots align with the two lanes and venom gathers at both fangs; close behind the hood or bait the spit away from the desired escape. | Upright forebody, broad patterned hood, compact rear coil, visibly distinct venom glands. |
| T4 | Horned Viper | Buried-Reach Champion | Conceals most of its body and attacks through alternating horn-marked emergence points while its exposed tail controls the safe route. | One horn breaks the surface before each head strike and the tail rattle points away from the real opening; follow the tail direction and punish the emerging neck. | Massive sand-adapted viper with paired eye horns, keeled scales, heavy tail, and multiple old shed scars. |
| T5 | Crown Serpent | Living-Coil Sovereign | Encircles the arena in layered body coils; lower Serpents occupy gaps and each broken coil exposes a crown scale that changes the boss's available attack. | Crown scales lift in the order of the next constriction and the corresponding lower Serpent becomes still; remove or displace it, then cross that loosened coil. | Colossal but plausible snake with crown-like occipital scales, long patterned body, four recognizable scar bands, and no humanoid torso. |

## Affinity construction contract

Each form receives five future individual images: Normal, Fire, Earth, Wind, and Water. The animal's
species, tier silhouette, movement, mechanic, telegraph, and exposed counterplay must remain readable
in every variant.

- **Normal:** native fur, feathers, scales, keratin, mud, saliva, dust, and ordinary motion. No
  unexplained elemental anatomy.
- **Fire:** localized combustion follows anatomy and attack flow—burning guard hairs, ember vents
  between scales, heated breath, smoke direction, and exposed cooling tissue. Never an orange wash.
- **Earth:** localized mineral accretion, sediment, cracked scutes, weighted claws, and detached
  stones follow load-bearing anatomy. Never replace the animal with a rock statue.
- **Wind:** directional fur or feather lift, pressure wakes, partial edge dissipation, and debris flow
  reveal movement. Never use transparency or pale recolor alone.
- **Water:** coherent wet anatomy, streaming fur or feathers, pressure sacs or channels, droplets,
  refraction, and a readable body core. Never turn the animal into an amorphous blue blob.

Every family packet adds anatomy-specific construction dimensions and forbiddens. Affinity is a
variant of the form, not another tier or species.
